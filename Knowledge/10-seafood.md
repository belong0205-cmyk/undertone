# Seafood — SITHCCC037 Prepare seafood dishes

## Classification
- Fin fish:
  - Round fish (eyes on both sides; two fillets): barramundi, snapper, salmon, ocean trout, flathead, whiting, bream, mackerel, tuna, kingfish, mulloway, coral trout, cod, blue-eye trevalla.
  - Flat fish (eyes on one side; four fillets): flounder, sole, John Dory (technically round-bodied but filleted similarly), halibut, turbot.
  - Oily fish (oil through the flesh, darker meat, rich in omega-3): salmon, ocean trout, tuna, mackerel, sardines, mullet, herring, kingfish.
  - White/lean fish (oil stored in the liver): snapper, flathead, whiting, barramundi, cod, dory, ling.
  - Freshwater (barramundi, Murray cod, trout, silver perch) vs saltwater.
- Shellfish:
  - Crustaceans (hard jointed outer shell, legs): prawns, rock lobster/crayfish, Moreton Bay bugs, Balmain bugs, crabs (mud crab, blue swimmer, spanner), marron, yabbies, scampi/langoustines.
  - Molluscs (soft body): univalves/gastropods (one shell: abalone, periwinkles, whelks), bivalves (two hinged shells: oysters — Sydney rock and Pacific, mussels, scallops, clams/vongole, pipis, cockles), cephalopods (no external shell: squid/calamari, octopus, cuttlefish).
- Other: sea urchin, sea cucumber, caviar/roe.
- Hard-shell: crustaceans with a hard outer shell (crab, lobster, bugs) and bivalves (oysters, mussels, clams). Soft-shell: soft-shell crab (just moulted, eaten whole, usually fried) and cephalopods (squid, octopus — no outer shell).
- Market forms and cuts: whole, gilled and gutted, dressed; fillet (whole side off the bone — pan-fry, grill, bake, poach); loin (thick boneless centre cut of large fish — tuna, swordfish, salmon — searing, sashimi, roasting); steak/cutlet (cross-cut with bone: darne from round fish, tronçon from flat fish — grill, barbecue, bake); portion (single-serve piece cut from a fillet, usually 160–200 g for a main, 80–100 g entrée); goujons (finger strips — crumbed or battered and fried); paupiettes (rolled fillets — poached); suprêmes (prime boneless skinless cut); butterflied (sardines, prawns); smoked, canned, frozen (IQF).

## Common Australian species — texture, flavour, best methods
| Fish | Notes |
|---|---|
| Barramundi | white, large moist flakes, mild buttery; pan-fry skin-on (crispy skin), grill, steam, bake |
| Snapper | white, medium-firm, sweet; bake or steam whole, pan-fry or grill fillets |
| Flathead | white, firm flakes, sweet, low oil; battered (fish and chips), crumbed, pan-fried |
| King George whiting | fine delicate flakes, sweet; pan-fry, crumb or batter, cook fast |
| John Dory | firm, fine white flesh, delicate sweet; thin fillets — pan-fry, poach, roast |
| Flounder (flat fish) | soft, delicate; pan-fry whole (meunière), grill, bake |
| Blue-eye trevalla, ling | firm, thick white flakes, mild; hold their shape — roast, pan-fry, curries, stews, fish and chips |
| Murray cod (farmed) | white, rich, firm; pan-fry, roast |
| Atlantic salmon, ocean trout | oily, orange-pink, rich; sashimi, pan-fry skin-on to medium, bake, cure (gravlax), smoke |
| Yellowtail kingfish | medium-oily, firm, clean taste; sashimi, ceviche, grill, pan-fry — don't overcook |
| Tuna | oily, dark red, meaty; sashimi or seared rare; dry if overcooked; keep cold (scombroid) |
| Mackerel, sardines, mullet | oily, strong flavour; grill, barbecue, bake, smoke; sardines butterflied; scombroid risk; large Spanish mackerel can carry ciguatera |

| Shellfish | Notes |
|---|---|
| Prawns (king, tiger, banana, school) | sweet, firm when cooked; devein; grill, stir-fry, poach (cocktail), tempura; 2–3 min until pink and C-shaped |
| Moreton Bay and Balmain bugs | sweet, rich tail meat like lobster; split and grill, or poach |
| Rock lobster (crayfish) | firm, sweet, rich, expensive; chill to kill humanely; grill split, poach, Thermidor, mornay |
| Mud crab | rich, sweet, meaty claws; steam or boil, chilli crab, salt and pepper |
| Blue swimmer crab | delicate sweet meat; steam or boil, picked meat for pasta, crab cakes, salads |
| Oysters | Sydney rock: smaller, creamy, strong mineral taste; Pacific: bigger, milder, salty; alive, shuck to order, serve natural on ice, Kilpatrick or mornay |
| Mussels | sweet, tender; scrub and debeard, steam 4–6 min until open (moules marinières); discard any open before or closed after cooking |
| Scallops | sweet, delicate; remove the side muscle; sear 30–60 s each side, ceviche, sashimi |
| Pipis, clams (vongole) | sweet, salty; purge sand in salted water; steam with garlic, chilli and wine; pasta vongole, XO pipis |
| Squid (calamari), cuttlefish | score and cook very fast (under 2 min: salt and pepper squid, grill) or braise slowly (30+ min); in between = rubbery |
| Octopus | tough raw; blanch, then simmer or braise 45–90 min until tender, then char-grill |
| Abalone | very firm, expensive; tenderise, slice thin and cook seconds, or braise long |

## Freshness and quality checks
| Fin fish fresh | Not fresh |
|---|---|
| bright, clear, bulging eyes | sunken, cloudy, grey eyes |
| bright red/pink moist gills | brown/grey, slimy gills |
| firm, elastic flesh that springs back | soft flesh, finger mark stays |
| tight, shiny scales, natural slime | dull, loose scales |
| fresh sea/ocean smell | strong fishy, ammonia or sour smell |
| fillets: translucent, moist, no gaping | dull, dry or discoloured, gaping |
- Crustaceans: heavy for size, shells intact and bright, legs/claws attached, no black discolouration at joints/heads of prawns (melanosis/black spot — cooking or sodium metabisulphite treatment), fresh smell; live ones lively.
- Bivalves (mussels, clams, oysters) must be alive when cooked raw/shucked: shells tightly closed or close when tapped ("tap test"); discard open ones that don't close, broken shells, and any that don't open after cooking (mussels/clams). Oysters: full, plump, moist, clear liquor, fresh smell.
- Cephalopods: shiny, moist skin, clear colour, sweet smell.
- Buy from reputable suppliers (seafood traceability; oysters from approved, monitored harvest areas). Check country of origin labelling for menus where required.

## Storage
- Fresh fish: ≤5°C (ideally 0–2°C) on ice in a perforated container so it drains (melting ice can wash away flavour and spread bacteria), covered, on the bottom shelf; use within 1–2 days.
- Live crustaceans: cool, moist, covered with damp cloth/hessian (not submerged in fresh water); some kept in seawater tanks. Live bivalves: in the cool room (about 2–5°C) covered with a damp cloth — not in sealed plastic (they suffocate) or in fresh water.
- Frozen seafood: −18°C; thaw in the fridge overnight on a tray; never refreeze.
- Smoked salmon and RTE seafood: Listeria risk — strict use-by, ≤5°C.

## Preparation techniques
- Scaling (tail to head), gutting (remove guts and bloodline, rinse), removing gills, trimming fins, skinning, filleting (round fish: two fillets; flat fish: four), pin-boning, portioning (e.g. 160–200 g main), cutting cutlets, butterflying (sardines, prawns), deveining prawns, debearding and scrubbing mussels just before cooking, shucking oysters (oyster knife, protective glove, hinge end), cleaning squid (head, quill, skin; score, cut rings), tenderising octopus, killing crustaceans humanely (chill to insensibility, then split or cook quickly), cleaning crab (apron, gills), splitting lobster and bugs.
- Fish stock from trimmings (bones of white non-oily fish; no gills/blood).

## Cooking methods and examples
- Seafood cooks quickly (little connective tissue) — overcooking makes it dry, tough and rubbery (squid/octopus: cook very fast — under 2 min — or very slowly).
- Fish is cooked when the flesh turns opaque and flakes easily (core around 60–63°C); prawns turn pink/orange and curl into a "C" (overcooked = "O").
- Delicate fish (whiting, flounder, John Dory, flathead, sole): use gentle methods — shallow poach, steam, bake en papillote, gentle pan-fry (meunière) with a light flour coating, or crumb/batter to protect the flesh; cook briefly, turn once with a fish slice, avoid hard grilling or boiling that breaks the flakes.
- Methods: pan-fry (meunière — floured, butter, lemon, parsley), grill/char-grill/barbecue (oily fish, prawns, octopus, whole fish), bake/roast (whole fish, en papillote, crusted fillets), shallow poach (sole Bonne Femme, fillets in fumet and wine, sauce made from the cooking liquor), deep poach (whole salmon in court bouillon), steam (Asian-style whole fish with ginger and shallot), deep fry (beer-battered fish, salt and pepper squid, tempura, crumbed calamari, whitebait), stew/braise (bouillabaisse, cioppino, seafood curries), stir-fry, sous vide, smoke (hot smoked vs cold smoked), cure (gravlax — salt, sugar, dill), raw (sashimi, oysters, ceviche — "cooked" by citrus acid, carpaccio, tartare — use sashimi-grade fish).
- Classic dishes: fish and chips, sole meunière, sole Bonne Femme, lobster Thermidor or mornay, moules marinières, oysters natural/Kilpatrick/mornay, garlic prawns, prawn cocktail (Marie Rose), paella, bouillabaisse (with rouille), seafood chowder, salt and pepper squid, chilli crab, fish en papillote, salmon with beurre blanc.
- Frying fish: dry, season, coat (flour → batter, or flour, egg, crumbs), fry at 175–180°C in small batches, drain on a rack, salt, serve at once. Greasy = oil too cool, fryer overloaded, old oil or thin batter → let the oil recover between batches, change oil. Stop sticking: clean, very hot, oiled grill or pan; dry the fish and oil it; skin-side down and don't move it until it releases.
- Sauces and accompaniments: lemon wedges, tartare sauce, beurre blanc, hollandaise, salsa verde, aioli/rouille, Marie Rose, sweet chilli, nam jim, dill/caper sauces.

## Seafood hazards
- Bacteria: Vibrio (raw seafood), Listeria (smoked/RTE seafood), norovirus and hepatitis A (raw oysters from polluted waters).
- Toxins: scombroid/histamine poisoning (tuna, mackerel, mahi-mahi not kept cold — histamine is not destroyed by cooking: keep cold from catch to cooking); ciguatera (large reef fish from tropical waters, e.g. Spanish mackerel, coral trout — toxin not destroyed by cooking); paralytic shellfish poisoning (algal blooms — buy from approved harvest areas).
- Parasites (e.g. anisakis worms) in raw fish — freezing (e.g. −20°C for 7 days or per supplier) kills them; buy sashimi-grade.
- Allergens: fish, crustacea and molluscs are three separate declarable allergens (Standard 1.2.3; PEAL from 25 Feb 2024) — prevent cross-contact (separate blue boards, oil, utensils, no shared fryer). Service obligations: declare them in menu descriptions or tell the customer on request, give accurate information (never guess — check the recipe and the chef), record the allergy on the docket, prepare the dish separately, and have the chef or supervisor confirm before it leaves the pass.
- Physical: bones, shell fragments.

## Sustainability
Choose sustainable species and certified sources (e.g. MSC/ASC labels, Australian sustainable seafood guides), respect seasons, minimum legal sizes and bag limits, use the whole fish (bones for stock, trimmings for fish cakes) to reduce waste; weigh portions and cut evenly from the fillet.
