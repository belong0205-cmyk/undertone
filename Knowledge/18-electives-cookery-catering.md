# Group A electives — Cookery and Catering

## SITHCCC038 Produce and serve food for buffets
- Buffet types: breakfast, lunch, finger food/cocktail, cold buffet (smorgasbord), hot buffet, carvery, dessert buffet, themed/international, staff/function buffets; self-serve vs attended (staff serve portions — better portion control and hygiene).
- Planning: number of guests, budget, theme, dietary needs (label each dish: name, V/VG/GF, allergens), menu balance, quantities (portion estimates per person), equipment (chafing dishes, bain-maries, cold plates/ice wells, sneeze guards, platters, risers, serving utensils for each dish), layout and flow (plates at the start, cold before hot, sauces next to their dish, two-sided for large numbers), replenishment plan.
- Presentation: height and levels, colour and texture contrast, garnishes, centrepieces (ice carvings, fruit displays, bread displays), clean edges, small platters replaced rather than topped up ("swap out"), portion-sized items.
- Food safety: hot food ≥60°C (preheat chafing dishes, never reheat in them), cold food ≤5°C (ice beds, refrigerated displays), sneeze guards, separate serving utensil for every dish (prevents allergen cross-contact), monitor temperatures and time (2h/4h rule — apply time as a control and record), don't mix new food with old on platters, discard leftovers that have been displayed beyond the time limit.
- Carvery: carve meat against the grain, portion consistently, keep warm under heat lamps, use carving board with drip channel, carving fork and knife.

## SITHCCC040 Prepare and serve cheese
- Classification: fresh/unripened (ricotta, cottage, cream cheese, mascarpone, bocconcini, mozzarella, feta, quark, chèvre frais), soft white-rind/bloomy (brie, camembert — Penicillium candidum), washed rind (taleggio, époisses, limburger — pungent, orange rind), semi-hard (gouda, edam, havarti, raclette, jarlsberg), hard (cheddar, gruyère, emmental), very hard/grana (parmesan/Parmigiano Reggiano, pecorino, grana padano), blue-veined (Roquefort — sheep, gorgonzola, stilton, Danish blue — Penicillium roqueforti), pasta filata (stretched curd: mozzarella, provolone, bocconcini, haloumi), goat and sheep milk cheeses.
- Made from milk coagulated with rennet (animal or microbial/vegetarian) or acid, curds separated from whey, salted, shaped, ripened/aged.
- Storage: wrapped in wax/cheese paper (breathes), not tightly in plastic for long-term; 2–8°C (cheese fridge often 8–12°C for aged cheeses); store strong cheeses separately; fresh cheeses ≤5°C and use quickly; Listeria risk in soft and fresh cheeses.
- Service: remove from fridge 30–60 minutes before serving (flavour at room temperature — but apply 2h/4h rule to soft/fresh cheeses); cut appropriate shapes (wedges keeping rind-to-centre proportions); separate knives for each cheese; arrange mild to strong (clockwise); 3–5 cheeses of different milks and textures; accompaniments: crackers, lavosh, bread, quince paste, fresh/dried fruit (grapes, figs, apple, pear), nuts, honey, chutney, muscatels, celery; portion about 30–50 g per cheese per person on a platter (approx. 100–150 g total for a cheese course).
- Cooking with cheese: melts best when grated and added at the end over low heat (high heat makes it stringy/oily); gratinate under a salamander.

## SITHCCC044 Prepare specialised food items
Specialised/advanced items such as forcemeats, mousselines, galantines, aspic, charcuterie, cured and smoked products, sushi, hand-made pasta, game, offal, specialist sauces — follow standard recipes, specialised equipment and food safety controls for higher-risk processes.

## SITHCCC032 Produce cook-chill and cook-freeze foods
- Cook-chill: food cooked, rapidly chilled (blast chiller) to ≤5°C (to 3°C or below within 90 minutes is a common blast-chill target in commercial systems; minimum legal 2h/4h rule), stored at 0–3/≤5°C for a limited shelf life (typically up to 5 days including day of cooking and service, per the system), then reheated (re-thermalised) to ≥75°C core.
- Cook-freeze: food cooked, blast-frozen (to −18°C or below quickly) and stored frozen for weeks/months; thawed in the fridge and re-thermalised.
- Sous-vide cook-chill and tumble chilling for large volumes.
- Controls: HACCP plan, labelled with product, production date, use-by date and batch number, temperature records at each stage, vacuum packs or covered GN trays, portioning before chilling, no re-chilling of re-thermalised food.
- Advantages: production in quiet periods, consistency, reduced waste, centralised production (hospitals, airlines, aged care, events).

## SITHCCC039 Produce pâtés and terrines
- Pâté: smooth, spreadable paste of liver/meat/fish with fat, seasonings and often alcohol (brandy, port); cooked; set and stored covered (clarified butter seal). Examples: chicken liver pâté, duck rillettes (shredded confit meat in fat), pâté en croûte (in pastry).
- Terrine: coarser forcemeat or layered ingredients cooked in a lined mould (lined with bacon/prosciutto, cling film or vegetables) in a bain-marie (~150–160°C oven until core reaches ~70–75°C), pressed and chilled, served sliced cold. Types: meat/game terrines (country-style), vegetable terrines set with gelatine, fish/seafood terrines (mousseline-based), pressed terrines (layered and weighted).
- Forcemeat (farce): ground meat + fat + seasoning + binder (panada, egg); types: straight, country-style (coarse, includes liver), gratin (part sautéed), mousseline (fine purée of fish/chicken + cream + egg white).
- Pink curing salt (sodium nitrite) used in some terrines for colour and safety — strictly measured.
- Food safety: high risk (Listeria, Clostridium) — cook to correct core temperature, chill rapidly, cover, label, short shelf life, keep ≤5°C, slice with clean knives.

## SITHCCC033 Re-thermalise chilled and frozen foods
Reheating (re-thermalising) cook-chill/cook-freeze food: thaw frozen food in the fridge (unless designed to be cooked from frozen), reheat rapidly (combi oven, steamer, microwave, re-therm trolley, water bath) to ≥75°C core within the time specified (often within 1–2 hours), check and record the temperature with a probe, hot-hold ≥60°C, serve promptly, never reheat more than once, discard leftovers.

## SITHCCC026 Package prepared foodstuffs
Packaging protects food from contamination and damage and extends shelf life (cling wrap, food-grade containers, vacuum or modified-atmosphere packs, sealed trays). Packaged food for sale needs a label (FSANZ Part 1.2): name, ingredients by weight, allergens in bold with a "Contains" statement (PEAL), use-by or best-before date, storage directions, business name and address, lot, net quantity, nutrition panel and country of origin where required. Package hygienically and quickly at the right temperature.

## SITXFSA007 Transport and store food
Transport food in clean, covered, food-grade containers; keep cold food ≤5°C (refrigerated vehicles, eskies with ice bricks), hot food ≥60°C (insulated hot boxes), frozen food kept frozen (3.2.2 cl 10); separate raw and RTE foods and chemicals; monitor and record temperatures and times; vehicles clean and pest-free; minimise transport time; label food with name, date and allergens; delivery documentation.
