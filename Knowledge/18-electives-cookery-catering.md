# Group A electives — Cookery and Catering

## SITHCCC038 Buffets and SITHCCC040 Cheese (short notes)
- Buffets: breakfast, finger food, cold, hot, carvery, dessert. Plan numbers, dietary labels (V/VG/GF, allergens), chafing dishes, ice wells, sneeze guards; hot ≥60°C (never reheat in chafing dishes), cold ≤5°C, never mix new food with old, swap small platters, discard displayed leftovers.
- Cheese: fresh (ricotta, feta), soft white-rind (brie), washed rind, semi-hard (gouda), hard (cheddar), very hard (parmesan), blue (Roquefort), haloumi. Store 2–8°C in cheese paper, fresh cheeses ≤5°C (Listeria); serve at room temperature (2h/4h), 3–5 cheeses mild to strong, 30–50 g per person.

## SITHCCC044 Prepare specialised food items
- Native ingredients: wattleseed (roasted, coffee-chocolate flavour — bread, ice cream), lemon myrtle (lemony leaf — fish, desserts, tea), finger lime (citrus "caviar" — seafood, oysters), Davidson plum (sour — jams, sauces), quandong (tart — pies, jams), Kakadu plum (very high vitamin C — sauces, desserts), macadamia, saltbush (salty leaf — lamb, bread), bush tomato (akudjura — spice rubs), pepperberry (hot pepper), warrigal greens (like spinach — blanch first, oxalates), kangaroo (very lean — cook rare or braise).
- Fermentation: bacteria or yeast turn sugars into acids, alcohol or gas — preserves food and adds flavour. Examples: sourdough, yoghurt, cheese, kimchi, sauerkraut, miso, soy sauce, fish sauce, vinegar, kombucha, salami, tempeh.
- Preserved foods are foods treated so they keep longer and stay safe. We remove water, add salt, sugar or acid, smoke, cook and seal, or use cold. Methods: pickling (vinegar or brine), curing, drying, smoking, confit (fat), jams (sugar), fermenting, bottling, freezing, vacuum packing.
- Aquatic foods beyond fish and shellfish: seaweeds (nori — sushi; kombu — dashi; wakame — salads, miso soup; agar comes from seaweed), samphire and sea succulents, sea urchin, roe and caviar, sea cucumber, yabbies, marron, eel.
- Aged meat: dry-ageing hangs large cuts unwrapped in a dedicated ageing fridge (about 1–3°C, controlled humidity and airflow) for weeks. Enzymes make the meat tender, and moisture loss concentrates the flavour. The dark crust is trimmed off. Wet-ageing is in vacuum bags. Benefits: tenderness and flavour. Risks: mould, bacteria, weight and trim loss, cost. Controls: a dedicated cabinet, temperature and humidity recorded, whole primals with fat cover only, HACCP, traceability.
- Hot smoking cooks and flavours the food (often 60–120°C): hot-smoked salmon, ham hock, brisket. It is ready to eat. Cold smoking only adds flavour, below about 30°C, so the food stays raw: smoked salmon, bacon, cheese, butter. I cure first, use frozen (parasite-safe) fish, keep it ≤5°C, vacuum pack and give a short use-by. The risks are Listeria and C. botulinum.
- Swollen vacuum-packed smoked fish: gas from bacterial growth (spoilage, possibly C. botulinum) — don't open or taste; isolate, label "do not use", discard or return to the supplier, record, tell the supervisor, check the batch and the fridge temperature.
- Curing: salt draws out moisture, sugar balances flavour, nitrite or nitrate (curing salt) keeps the pink colour and stops C. botulinum — a permitted food additive with legal limits (Std 1.3.1, Schedule 15), so weigh it exactly to the standard recipe. Dry cure or brine.
- HACCP for specialised foods: critical points such as curing salt weight, smoking or cooking temperature, ageing temperature and humidity, fermentation pH and time, cooling; monitor, record, correct, verify.
- Storage and labels: sealed and labelled; most pickles, ferments and cured meats ≤5°C once opened; clean utensils only; garlic or herbs in oil ≤5°C with a short life (botulism). Label: name, date made, use-by, batch, ingredients and allergens, storage directions, initials.
- Ferment gone wrong: fuzzy or coloured mould (pink, black, green), slime, rotten smell instead of a clean sour one, odd colour, mushy texture, bulging lids, pH not dropping → discard.
- High-risk customers: avoid raw, cold-smoked and unpasteurised fermented foods for pregnant, elderly and immunocompromised guests; offer cooked options; check native ingredients for allergens and prepare them correctly; explain clearly.

## Other Group A electives (short notes)
- SITHCCC032/033 cook-chill and cook-freeze: I cook, then blast-chill (often to 3°C within 90 min; Code minimum 60→21°C in 2 h, 21→5°C in 4 h). I store at 0–5°C for up to about 5 days, or blast-freeze to −18°C. I keep HACCP records and labels. I reheat fast to ≥75°C core, hot-hold ≥60°C, reheat once and never re-chill.
- SITHCCC039 pâtés and terrines: a pâté is a smooth paste of liver, meat or fish with fat. A terrine is forcemeat in a lined mould, cooked in a bain-marie to 70–75°C core, pressed, chilled and sliced. Listeria is the risk, so I chill fast, label and keep a short shelf life. SITHPAT014 yeast products: see file 11.
- SITHCCC026 packaging: food-grade wrap, containers, vacuum or MAP; labels for sale (FSANZ Part 1.2): name, ingredients, allergens (PEAL), use-by/best-before, storage, business details, lot, net quantity.
