# Group A electives — Cookery and Catering

## SITHCCC038 Produce and serve food for buffets
Types: breakfast, finger food, cold, hot, carvery, dessert, themed; self-serve or attended (better portion control and hygiene). Plan guests, budget, dietary labels (V/VG/GF, allergens), quantities, equipment (chafing dishes, ice wells, sneeze guards) and flow (plates first, cold before hot, sauce next to its dish). Present with height and colour; swap small platters rather than topping up. Safety: hot ≥60°C (never reheat in chafing dishes), cold ≤5°C, a utensil for every dish, 2h/4h rule recorded, never mix new food with old, discard displayed leftovers. Carvery: carve against the grain under heat lamps.

## SITHCCC040 Prepare and serve cheese
Types: fresh (ricotta, feta, mozzarella, mascarpone), soft white-rind (brie, camembert), washed rind (taleggio), semi-hard (gouda, havarti), hard (cheddar, gruyère), very hard (parmesan, pecorino), blue (Roquefort, gorgonzola, stilton), pasta filata (mozzarella, haloumi). Made by coagulating milk with rennet (animal or microbial) or acid. Store in cheese paper at 2–8°C, fresh cheeses ≤5°C (Listeria). Serve at room temperature (out 30–60 min; 2h/4h for soft cheeses), 3–5 cheeses mild to strong, separate knives, about 30–50 g each per person, with crackers, bread, quince paste, fruit, nuts. In cooking, grate and melt over low heat.

## SITHCCC044 Prepare specialised food items
- Native ingredients: wattleseed (roasted, coffee-chocolate flavour — bread, ice cream), lemon myrtle (lemony leaf — fish, desserts, tea), finger lime (citrus "caviar" — seafood, oysters), Davidson plum (sour — jams, sauces), quandong (tart — pies, jams), Kakadu plum (very high vitamin C — sauces, desserts), macadamia, saltbush (salty leaf — lamb, bread), bush tomato (akudjura — spice rubs), pepperberry (hot pepper), warrigal greens (like spinach — blanch first, oxalates), kangaroo (very lean — cook rare or braise).
- Fermentation: bacteria or yeast turn sugars into acids, alcohol or gas — preserves food and adds flavour. Examples: sourdough, yoghurt, cheese, kimchi, sauerkraut, miso, soy sauce, fish sauce, vinegar, kombucha, salami, tempeh.
- Preserving: pickling (vinegar or brine), curing, drying, smoking, confit (fat), sugar (jams), fermenting, bottling, freezing, vacuum packing.
- Aquatic foods beyond fish and shellfish: seaweeds (nori — sushi; kombu — dashi; wakame — salads, miso soup; agar comes from seaweed), samphire and sea succulents, sea urchin, roe and caviar, sea cucumber, yabbies, marron, eel.
- Aged meat: dry-aged = large cuts unwrapped in a dedicated ageing fridge (about 1–3°C, controlled humidity and airflow) for weeks; enzymes tenderise and moisture loss concentrates flavour; the dark crust is trimmed. Wet-aged = in vacuum bags. Benefits: tenderness, flavour. Risks: mould and bacterial growth, weight and trim loss, cost. Controls: dedicated cabinet, temperature and humidity monitored and recorded, whole primals with fat cover only, trim the crust, HACCP, traceability.
- Hot smoking cooks and flavours (smoker hot enough to cook the food, often 60–120°C — hot-smoked salmon, ham hock, brisket); ready to eat. Cold smoking flavours only, below about 30°C, so food stays raw (smoked salmon, bacon, cheese, butter, salt): cure first, use parasite-safe (frozen) fish, keep ≤5°C, vacuum pack, short use-by — risks are Listeria and C. botulinum.
- Swollen vacuum-packed smoked fish: gas from bacterial growth (spoilage, possibly C. botulinum) — don't open or taste; isolate, label "do not use", discard or return to the supplier, record, tell the supervisor, check the batch and the fridge temperature.
- Curing: salt draws out moisture, sugar balances flavour, nitrite or nitrate (curing salt) keeps the pink colour and stops C. botulinum — a permitted food additive with legal limits (Std 1.3.1, Schedule 15), so weigh it exactly to the standard recipe. Dry cure or brine.
- HACCP for specialised foods: critical points such as curing salt weight, smoking or cooking temperature, ageing temperature and humidity, fermentation pH and time, cooling; monitor, record, correct, verify.
- Storage and labels: sealed and labelled; most pickles, ferments and cured meats ≤5°C once opened; clean utensils only; garlic or herbs in oil ≤5°C with a short life (botulism). Label: name, date made, use-by, batch, ingredients and allergens, storage directions, initials.
- Ferment gone wrong: fuzzy or coloured mould (pink, black, green), slime, rotten smell instead of a clean sour one, odd colour, mushy texture, bulging lids, pH not dropping → discard.
- High-risk customers: avoid raw, cold-smoked and unpasteurised fermented foods for pregnant, elderly and immunocompromised guests; offer cooked options; check native ingredients for allergens and prepare them correctly; explain clearly.

## SITHCCC032 Produce cook-chill and cook-freeze foods
Cook-chill: cook, blast-chill fast (often to 3°C within 90 minutes; the Code minimum is 60→21°C in 2 h and 21→5°C in a further 4 h), store at 0–5°C for a short life (often up to 5 days, per the system), re-thermalise to ≥75°C core. Cook-freeze: blast-freeze to −18°C or below, store for weeks or months, thaw in the fridge, re-thermalise. Controls: HACCP plan, labels (product, production and use-by date, batch), temperature records at each stage, portion before chilling, never re-chill re-thermalised food. Used by hospitals, airlines, aged care and events.

## SITHCCC039 Produce pâtés and terrines
Pâté: smooth paste of liver, meat or fish with fat and seasoning, sealed with clarified butter (chicken liver pâté, rillettes). Terrine: coarse forcemeat or layers in a lined mould, cooked in a bain-marie (~150–160°C oven to a 70–75°C core), pressed, chilled, sliced. Forcemeat: meat + fat + seasoning + binder (panada, egg); mousseline = fine purée with cream and egg white. Curing salt measured exactly. High risk (Listeria, Clostridium): cook to core, chill fast, label, short shelf life, ≤5°C.

## SITHPAT014 Produce yeast-based bakery products
See file 11 (breads and yeast products): croissants and Danish (laminated yeast doughs), brioche, doughnuts, bread rolls, buns, pizza, focaccia, savarin and babas (soaked in rum syrup).

## SITHCCC033 Re-thermalise chilled and frozen foods
Reheating (re-thermalising) cook-chill/cook-freeze food: thaw frozen food in the fridge (unless designed to be cooked from frozen), reheat rapidly (combi oven, steamer, microwave, re-therm trolley, water bath) to ≥75°C core within the time specified (often within 1–2 hours), check and record the temperature with a probe, hot-hold ≥60°C, serve promptly, never reheat more than once, discard leftovers.

## SITHCCC026 Package prepared foodstuffs
Packaging protects food and extends shelf life (cling wrap, food-grade containers, vacuum or modified-atmosphere packs). Labels for sale (FSANZ Part 1.2): name, ingredients, allergens in bold + "Contains" (PEAL), use-by or best-before, storage directions, business name and address, lot, net quantity, nutrition panel, country of origin where required.

## SITXFSA007 Transport and store food
Clean, covered food-grade containers; cold ≤5°C, hot ≥60°C, frozen kept frozen (3.2.2 cl 10); separate raw, ready-to-eat and chemicals; monitor and record temperatures and times; clean, pest-free vehicles; label name, date, allergens.
