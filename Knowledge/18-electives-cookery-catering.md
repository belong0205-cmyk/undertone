# Group A electives — Cookery and Catering

## SITHCCC038 Buffets and SITHCCC040 Cheese (short notes)
- Buffets: plan numbers, dietary labels (V/VG/GF, allergens), chafing dishes, ice wells, sneeze guards. Hot ≥60°C (never reheat in chafing dishes), cold ≤5°C. Never mix new food with old, swap small platters, discard displayed leftovers.
- Cheese: fresh (ricotta, feta), soft white-rind (brie), washed rind, semi-hard (gouda). Hard (cheddar), very hard (parmesan), blue (Roquefort), haloumi. Store 2–8°C in cheese paper, fresh cheeses ≤5°C (Listeria). Serve at room temperature (2h/4h).

## SITHCCC044 Prepare specialised food items
- Native ingredients: wattleseed is roasted and tastes of coffee and chocolate. It goes in bread, cakes and ice cream. Lemon myrtle is a lemony leaf for fish, desserts and tea. Finger lime is citrus "caviar" for seafood and oysters. Davidson plum is sour, for jams and sauces. Quandong is tart, for pies and jams. Kakadu plum has very high vitamin C, for sauces and desserts. Macadamia is a native nut. Saltbush is a salty leaf for lamb and bread. Bush tomato (akudjura) goes in spice rubs. Pepperberry is a hot pepper. Warrigal greens are like spinach but have oxalates, so I blanch them first. Kangaroo is very lean, so I cook it rare or braise it.
- Fermentation: bacteria or yeast turn sugars into acids, alcohol or gas. It preserves food and adds flavour. Examples: sourdough, yoghurt, cheese, kimchi, sauerkraut, miso, soy sauce, fish sauce, vinegar, kombucha.
- Preserved foods are treated so they keep longer and stay safe. We remove water, add salt, sugar or acid, or smoke. Or we cook and seal, or use cold. Methods: pickling (vinegar or brine), curing, drying, smoking, confit (fat), jams (sugar). Also fermenting, bottling, freezing, vacuum packing.
- Aquatic foods beyond fish and shellfish: seaweeds (nori — sushi; kombu — dashi; wakame — salads, miso soup; agar comes from seaweed), samphire and sea succulents, sea urchin, roe and caviar, sea cucumber, yabbies, marron, eel.
- Aged meat: dry-ageing hangs large cuts unwrapped in an ageing fridge for weeks. About 1–3°C, with controlled humidity and airflow. Enzymes make the meat tender; moisture loss concentrates the flavour. I trim off the dark crust. Wet-ageing is in vacuum bags. Risks: mould, bacteria, weight and trim loss, cost. Controls: a dedicated cabinet, temperature and humidity records, HACCP, traceability. Whole primals with fat cover only.
- Hot smoking cooks and flavours the food (often 60–120°C): hot-smoked salmon, ham hock, brisket. It is ready to eat. Cold smoking only adds flavour, below about 30°C, so the food stays raw. Examples: smoked salmon, bacon, cheese. I cure first and use frozen (parasite-safe) fish. I keep it ≤5°C, vacuum pack and give a short use-by. The risks are Listeria and C. botulinum.
- Swollen vacuum-packed smoked fish: gas from bacterial growth (spoilage, possibly C. botulinum). Don't open or taste it. Isolate it, label "do not use", discard or return it, record it. Tell the supervisor and check the batch and the fridge temperature.
- Curing: salt draws out moisture and sugar balances flavour. Nitrite or nitrate (curing salt) keeps the pink colour and stops C. botulinum. It is a permitted additive with legal limits (Std 1.3.1). I weigh it exactly. Dry cure or brine.
- HACCP for specialised foods: critical points such as curing salt weight, smoking or cooking temperature, ageing temperature and humidity, fermentation pH and time, cooling; monitor, record, correct, verify.
- Storage and labels: sealed and labelled; most pickles, ferments and cured meats ≤5°C once opened; clean utensils only; garlic or herbs in oil ≤5°C with a short life (botulism). Label: name, date made, use-by, batch, ingredients and allergens, storage directions, initials.
- Ferment gone wrong: fuzzy or coloured mould (pink, black, green), slime, rotten smell instead of a clean sour one, odd colour, mushy texture, bulging lids, pH not dropping → discard.
- High-risk customers: pregnant, elderly and immunocompromised guests. I avoid raw, cold-smoked and unpasteurised fermented foods for them. I offer cooked options, check native ingredients for allergens and explain clearly.

## Other Group A electives (short notes)
- SITHCCC032/033 cook-chill and cook-freeze: I cook, then blast-chill, often to 3°C within 90 min. I store at 0–5°C for up to 5 days, or blast-freeze to −18°C. I reheat fast to ≥75°C core, hot-hold ≥60°C, reheat once and never re-chill.
- SITHCCC039 pâtés and terrines. A pâté is a smooth paste of liver, meat or fish with fat. A terrine is forcemeat in a lined mould. I cook it in a bain-marie to 70–75°C core. Then I press, chill and slice it. Listeria is the risk. So I chill fast, label and keep a short shelf life. SITHPAT014 yeast products: see file 11.
- SITHCCC026 packaging: food-grade wrap, containers, vacuum or MAP. Labels for sale: name, ingredients, allergens (PEAL), use-by/best-before. Also storage, business details, lot, net quantity.
