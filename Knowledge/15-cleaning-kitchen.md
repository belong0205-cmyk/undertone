# Clean kitchen premises and equipment — SITHKOP009

## Definitions
- Cleaning: removing visible dirt, food residue, grease and debris using detergent and water (and physical action). Clean = clean to touch, no visible matter or bad smell (Std 3.1.1).
- Sanitising: applying heat, chemicals, or heat and chemicals to a clean surface or utensil so micro-organisms are reduced to a level that does not compromise food safety (3.2.2 cl 20(2)). It reduces germs; it does not sterilise. Clean first — sanitisers don't work on dirty surfaces.
- Disinfecting kills most micro-organisms on non-food surfaces (toilets, bins, drains) with stronger chemicals. Detergent removes grease and dirt: alkaline for grease, acid descaler for limescale. No abrasives on stainless steel or non-stick. Sanitisers: chlorine (bleach), quats, iodine, 70% alcohol, two-in-one detergent-sanitisers. With bleach I check the concentration; it is corrosive. With two-in-one products I still clean first when heavily soiled.
- Why cleaning and sanitising matter: they remove food, grease and moisture. Bacteria and pests live on these. They stop cross-contamination and food poisoning. They protect allergy customers. They keep equipment working and safe (WHS). They meet the law (Std 3.2.2 cl 19–20; fines or closure). They give customers confidence in the business.

## Cleaning and sanitising steps (6-step method)
1. Pre-clean: scrape, wipe or rinse off loose food and debris.
2. Wash: hot water + detergent to remove grease and dirt (scrub).
3. Rinse: clean water to remove detergent and loosened dirt.
4. Sanitise: heat (hot water at 77°C for 30 seconds), a chemical sanitiser at the manufacturer's concentration and contact time (e.g. bleach 100 ppm in cold or 50 ppm in warm water; 70% alcohol), or both (a dishwasher's sanitising cycle) (FSANZ; Safe Food Australia App. 6).
5. Final rinse (if the sanitiser label requires).
6. Dry: air-dry (preferred) or single-use paper towel — tea towels can recontaminate.

## When to clean and sanitise
Code: eating and drinking utensils clean and sanitary immediately before each use; food contact surfaces whenever food on them is likely to be contaminated (3.2.2 cl 20(1)); premises and equipment with no build-up of garbage, food waste, dirt or grease (cl 19).
Before starting food preparation; after each task (especially raw meat/poultry/seafood); between raw and RTE foods; after spills; at the end of the shift; according to the cleaning schedule; after deliveries; when interrupted (e.g. after touching the bin).
- Preventing cross-contamination while cleaning: I move or cover food first. I clean from the cleanest to the dirtiest area and from high to low. Floors, bins, drains and toilets come last. I use separate colour-coded cloths, mops and buckets for food surfaces, floors and toilets. I never spray or splash near food or clean dishes. I change dirty water and cloths. I keep chemicals away from food. After the clean I wash my hands, change gloves and sanitise food-contact surfaces.

## Cleaning schedule
Written schedule: what, how (method, chemical, dilution, PPE), when (after use, daily, weekly, monthly), who, and sign-off checked by a supervisor. E.g. benches, boards, utensils after each use; floors daily; fridges daily wipe, weekly deep clean; ovens, fryers, hood filters weekly; cool rooms and grease trap monthly; pest control by contractor.

## Equipment cleaning
- Large equipment: switch off and isolate (unplug, gas off), let it cool, dismantle removable parts (slicer blade guard, mixer bowl, food processor blades), wash/rinse/sanitise parts, wipe fixed parts, dry, reassemble correctly, test, report faults, follow manufacturer instructions.
- Slicer: set blade to zero, unplug, cut-resistant gloves, wipe from the centre outwards.
- Knives and sharp tools: I wash one at a time. Blade pointing away, handle in my hand. I never drop them into a sink of water or leave them there. I wash, rinse, sanitise and dry at once (rust). I store them in a rack, guard or magnetic strip.
- Deep fryer: I switch it off and let the oil cool completely. I never drain hot oil. I filter daily. I drain the oil into the used-oil drum for recycling. I boil it out with fryer cleaner as the manufacturer says. I rinse, then dry it fully before refilling, because water and hot oil erupt. PPE: apron, heat-resistant gloves, goggles.
- Dishwashers: I scrape and pre-rinse, load correctly and use the sanitising cycle. I check the wash and rinse temperatures or chemical levels. I clean the filters and arms daily and check detergent and rinse-aid.
- Pot washing (manual): three-sink method — wash (detergent), rinse, sanitise — then air dry.
- Dirty equipment stored beside clean dishes during service: I speak calmly. I move the dirty items to the dirty side. I re-wash and sanitise any clean items that were touched or splashed. I remind the person of the one-way flow, dirty → clean. I help if they are swamped. I tell the supervisor so the setup or training is fixed.
- Chopping boards: scrub, sanitise; replace when deeply scored (bacteria hide in grooves).
- Cloths: use colour-coded single-use or launder daily; keep separate cloths for food and non-food areas; dispose of single-use wipes.

## Chemical safety
- Store in original labelled containers, away from food, in a designated chemical store (low shelves, not above food).
- Read the label and SDS; use the correct dilution (dosing systems), never mix chemicals (bleach + ammonia or acids → toxic gases).
- PPE for cleaning: rubber gloves, a waterproof apron and enclosed non-slip shoes. Goggles or a face shield when splashing or decanting. A mask where the SDS requires one. Heat-resistant gloves for fryers and ovens.
- Chemical spill on the floor: I warn others and put up a wet-floor sign. I put on PPE and check the SDS. I stop the source. I contain and absorb it with the spill kit. I never hose it into the drain unless the SDS allows. I wipe and rinse so nobody slips. I ventilate. I bin the waste as the SDS says. I wash my hands, then report and record it.
- Wrong chemical on a food surface (degreaser, drain cleaner). The residue poisons and taints food. It corrodes stainless steel and non-stick coatings. Fumes burn eyes and lungs. It may not sanitise, so bacteria survive. I use only food-safe products at the label dilution. I rinse where the label says. If it happens, I rinse, re-sanitise and throw out food that touched it.
- Unsanitised thermometer: I probe cooked chicken, then salad. Bacteria and allergens move from one food to the other (cross-contamination). The salad is eaten raw, so nothing kills them. Customers can get food poisoning. I wash and sanitise the probe between every food. I use an alcohol wipe or sanitiser and let it dry.
- Decant only into labelled spray bottles; never use food containers for chemicals.
- First aid: follow SDS — rinse eyes/skin with water for 15–20 min, Poisons Information Centre 13 11 26, call 000 in an emergency.

## Waste management
- Separate waste streams: general waste, recyclables (glass, cardboard, plastics, cans), organic/food waste (compost), used oil (recycled), hazardous waste (chemicals, batteries, sharps), grease trap waste (licensed contractor).
- Bins: lined, lidded, foot-operated, emptied regularly and cleaned/sanitised; bin areas kept clean to avoid pests and odours.
- Broken glass: sweep with dustpan, wrap/container labelled "broken glass", never put hands in bins.
- Follow local council and environmental regulations; reduce, reuse, recycle.

## Pest control
Signs: droppings, gnaw marks, grease trails, chewed packaging, a bad smell. Also live or dead insects, eggs, nests. If I see them, I tell the supervisor at once. I isolate and throw out any food or packaging that may be contaminated. I clean and sanitise the area. I never spray pesticide near food. I record it in the pest log and call the licensed pest controller. Prevention: keep it clean, food covered and off the floor, bins closed. Seal gaps, fit screens and door seals, check deliveries (3.2.2 cl 24).

## Environmental and efficiency practices when cleaning
Use correct chemical dilutions (no overuse), environmentally friendly products where possible, conserve water (don't leave taps running, full dishwasher loads), switch off equipment, recycle packaging.
