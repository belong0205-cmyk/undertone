# Work health and safety — SITXWHS005 Participate in safe work practices (also SITXWHS006 basics; SITXWHS007 Implement and monitor WHS practices is the Certificate IV unit)

## Legislation and duties
- Model Work Health and Safety (WHS) Act 2011 + WHS Regulations + Codes of Practice (Safe Work Australia). Victoria: Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004. Regulators: SafeWork NSW, WorkSafe Vic/Qld/WA, SafeWork SA, etc.
- PCBU (person conducting a business or undertaking, i.e. the employer/business) has the primary duty of care: safe workplace, safe plant and systems of work, safe use/storage of substances, adequate facilities, information/training/supervision, monitoring health and conditions, consultation with workers.
- Officers (directors/managers) must exercise due diligence.
- Workers must: take reasonable care for their own health and safety and that of others, comply with reasonable instructions, follow policies and procedures, use PPE provided, report hazards, incidents and injuries.
- Others at the workplace (visitors, customers, contractors) must take reasonable care too.
- Consultation: through health and safety representatives (HSR), health and safety committees, toolbox talks, staff meetings.
- Workers have the right to cease unsafe work if there is a serious risk.
- Workers compensation and return-to-work: report injuries promptly; employer must hold workers compensation insurance.
- Notifiable incidents (death, serious injury/illness, dangerous incident) must be reported to the regulator immediately and the site preserved.

## Key terms
- Hazard: anything with the potential to cause harm (hot oil, wet floor, sharp knife).
- Risk: the likelihood that harm will occur and how severe it would be.
- Risk control: action to eliminate or minimise the risk.
- Incident: unplanned event that caused or could have caused injury/damage. Near miss: incident with no injury.
- SDS: Safety Data Sheet from the manufacturer or importer for each hazardous chemical: identity, hazards, safe handling and storage, PPE, first aid, spills and fire. The PCBU must get the current SDS and keep it readily accessible; manufacturers review it at least every 5 years (model WHS Regulations r 330, 344).

## Risk management process
1. Identify hazards (inspections, checklists, incident reports, consultation, SDS, manufacturer instructions).
2. Assess risks (likelihood × consequence, risk matrix: low / medium / high / extreme).
3. Control risks using the hierarchy of controls.
4. Review controls (after incidents, changes, regularly).

## Hierarchy of control (most effective to least; model WHS Regulations r 36, Safe Work Australia Code of Practice: How to manage WHS risks)
1. Elimination — remove the hazard (e.g. remove a broken slicer from use).
2. Substitution — replace with something safer (less toxic cleaning chemical, lighter containers).
3. Isolation — separate people from the hazard (guards, barriers, lock-out).
4. Engineering controls — trolleys, mechanical aids, extraction hoods, non-slip flooring, machine guards, auto shut-off.
5. Administrative controls — procedures, training, signage, job rotation, rosters, cleaning schedules, "wet floor" signs.
6. PPE — gloves, oven mitts, non-slip enclosed shoes, aprons, chain-mail/cut-resistant gloves, goggles, freezer jackets.
(r 36 order: 1 eliminate the risk; 2 if not reasonably practicable, minimise it by substitution, isolation and/or engineering controls; 3 then administrative controls for any remaining risk; 4 then PPE. Administrative controls and PPE are the least effective and are used together with higher controls.)

## Common kitchen hazards and controls
| Hazard | Controls |
|---|---|
| Slips, trips, falls (wet/greasy floors, clutter, cords) | clean spills immediately, wet floor signs, non-slip shoes and mats, keep walkways clear, good lighting, don't run |
| Cuts (knives, slicers, broken glass, can lids) | sharp knives, correct cutting technique, damp cloth under board, carry knives point down by your side, never leave knives in sink, machine guards, cut-resistant gloves, dustpan for glass |
| Burns and scalds (hot oil, steam, ovens, hot pans) | dry oven cloths, call "hot behind/coming through", pan handles turned inward, open lids away from you, lower food into oil away from you, don't overfill fryers, let oil cool before filtering |
| Manual handling (lifting stock, pots) | assess the load, plan the lift, get help or use trolleys, bend knees, keep back straight and load close, avoid twisting, store heavy items between knee and shoulder height (Code of Practice: Hazardous manual tasks) |
| Electrical | check cords/plugs, keep away from water, tag-and-test, don't use faulty equipment, report and tag out |
| Hazardous chemicals | read labels and SDS, use correct dilution, PPE, never mix chemicals (e.g. bleach + ammonia/acid → toxic gas), store away from food, original labelled containers with GHS labels (Code of Practice: Managing risks of hazardous chemicals in the workplace) |
| Fire | clean grease from hoods/filters, never leave oil unattended, know extinguisher types and evacuation routes |
| Gas | check for leaks, ventilation, turn off when not in use; if leak: no flames/switches, ventilate, evacuate, call gas emergency |
| Heat and fatigue/stress | ventilation, breaks, water, reasonable rosters |
| Cool rooms | inside release handle, freezer jacket, limit time |
| Workplace violence/bullying | policies, report to manager, support services |

## Fires — classes and extinguishers (Australia)
- Class A: ordinary combustibles (paper, wood, cloth). Class B: flammable liquids. Class C: flammable gases. Class D: combustible metals. Class E: electrical equipment. Class F: cooking oils and fats.
- Water (all red): A only. Foam (blue band): A, B. Dry chemical powder ABE (white band): A, B, C, E. Carbon dioxide CO2 (black band): E (and small B). Wet chemical (oatmeal band): F (and A) — the right extinguisher for deep-fryer/cooking oil fires.
- Fire blanket: smother small pan/fat fires and clothing fires.
- Never use water on an oil fire (it explodes into steam and spreads burning oil) or on electrical fires.
- Pan fire procedure: turn off heat/gas if safe, cover with lid or fire blanket, leave it covered, do not move the pan, raise alarm.
- PASS for extinguishers: Pull pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze handle, Sweep side to side.
- Emergency procedures: know exits, assembly area, warden instructions, evacuation diagrams; raise alarm, call 000, help others if safe, don't use lifts, don't return to the building.

## Incident reporting and first aid
- Report all hazards, incidents, near misses and injuries to the supervisor immediately and complete an incident/injury report form (what, where, when, who, witnesses, actions taken).
- Burns: cool under cool running water for 20 minutes, remove jewellery, cover with non-stick dressing/cling wrap, don't apply ice, butter or creams, seek medical help for large/deep burns.
- Bleeding: apply direct pressure, elevate, cover with dressing; seek help if severe.
- Know who the first aider is and where the first-aid kit is.
- First aid DRSABCD (HLTAID011): Danger, Response, Send for help (000), Airway, Breathing, CPR (30 compressions : 2 breaths), Defibrillation (AED).

## Safe work practices for cooks
- Follow equipment manufacturer instructions and workplace procedures; only use equipment you are trained on; report faulty equipment and tag it "Out of service".
- Wear correct uniform: chef jacket (long sleeves protect from burns), long pants, apron, enclosed non-slip shoes, hat/hair net.
- Keep the work area clean and organised ("clean as you go").
- Communicate: "behind", "hot", "sharp", "corner".
- Manage personal wellbeing: breaks, hydration, fatigue, stress, rest between shifts.
