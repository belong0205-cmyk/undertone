# SIT30821 Certificate III in Commercial Cookery — overview and quick facts

Australian qualification: answers use Australian standards and terms (FSANZ Food Standards Code, WHS laws, Celsius, metric, GST). Numbers differ slightly between RTOs, textbooks and workplaces: where a range is given, the middle is the safe answer, and "follow workplace procedures / the standard recipe" is always correct to add.

## Qualification
- The qualification has 25 units: 20 core and 5 electives. SITHCCC043: I must work at least 48 food service shifts in a commercial kitchen. I record them in a logbook.

## Numbers most often asked (Australia)
### Temperature control
- Temperature danger zone: 5°C to 60°C (bacteria multiply fastest around 37°C). The Code's "temperature control" = 5°C or below, or 60°C or above (FSANZ 3.2.2 cl 1).
- Cold food: 5°C or below (cool room / fridge ideally 1–4°C) when receiving, storing and displaying PHF (3.2.2 cl 5(3), 6(2), 8(5)). Frozen food must be received frozen and stay frozen (cl 5(4), 6(2)(b)); −18°C or below is the usual freezer setting (guidance, not a Code figure).
- Hot food: 60°C or above when receiving, hot-holding, displaying and transporting (cl 5(3), 8(5), 10).
- 2-hour/4-hour rule for ready-to-eat potentially hazardous food that has been between 5°C and 60°C:
  Under 2 hours in total: I can use it or chill it again. 2 to 4 hours: I must use it now and not chill it again. Over 4 hours: I throw it out (Safe Food Australia, App. 2). Example: sandwiches come out of the fridge at 10:00. I can chill them again until 12:00. I must use them between 12:00 and 14:00. After 14:00 I throw them out. The times add up over the day.
- Thermometer: accurate to ±1°C (3.2.2 cl 22). Check in ice slurry (0°C) and boiling water (100°C at sea level); sanitise the probe between foods.
- Thawing: in the fridge (best), under cold running water in sealed packaging, or in the microwave and cook immediately. Never thaw at room temperature. Do not refreeze thawed raw food unless it is cooked first.
### Hygiene
- Hand washing (3.2.2 cl 15(4)): soap and warm running water, clean hands thoroughly (guidance: at least 20 seconds), dry on a single-use towel. When: before handling food, after the toilet, after raw food before ready-to-eat food, after coughing, sneezing, eating, smoking or touching hair or body (cl 15(2)–(3)).
### Kitchen numbers
- Deep-frying oil 160–190°C (most foods 175–180°C). Change oil when dark, foaming, smoking at normal temperature or smelling rancid.
- Roux: equal weights of fat and flour. Thin sauce ≈ 50 g roux per 500 mL liquid, coating sauce ≈ 100 g per 500 mL, panada (binding) ≈ 200 g per 500 mL (roux = fat + flour combined).
- Sugar stages: thread 106–112°C, soft ball 112–116°C, firm ball 118–120°C, hard ball 121–130°C, soft crack 132–143°C, hard crack 149–154°C, caramel 160–177°C.
- Chocolate tempering (dark): melt 45–50°C, cool to 27–28°C, rework to 31–32°C. Milk: 40–45 → 26–27 → 29–30°C. White: 40 → 25–26 → 28–29°C.
- Yeast dough ideal temperature ~26–28°C; yeast dies around 55–60°C.

## Chopping board colour coding (common in Australia; follow workplace system)
Red = raw meat; Yellow = raw poultry; Blue = raw seafood; Green = fruit and vegetables/salad; White = dairy and bakery; Brown = cooked meats.
Purple is often used for allergen-free (e.g. gluten-free) preparation.

## Key legislation and standards
- Each state has a Food Act. South Australia: Food Act 2001, enforced by SA Health and local councils. NSW: Food Act 2003. Victoria: Food Act 1984.
- FSANZ Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code, Chapter 3 (Australia only):
  Std 3.1.1 Interpretation and application; Std 3.2.1 Food Safety Programs (only where state law requires); Std 3.3.1 Food Safety Programs for Food Service to Vulnerable Persons (hospitals, aged care, child care);
  Std 3.2.2 Food Safety Practices and General Requirements; Std 3.2.2A Food Safety Management Tools (in force from 8 December 2023);
  Std 3.2.3 Food Premises and Equipment.
- Std 1.2.3 and Schedule 9: Plain English Allergen Labelling (PEAL), in force from 25 February 2024 — allergens declared by plain-English names in bold, plus a "Contains" statement.
- Employment law: Fair Work Act 2009 and anti-discrimination laws. Awards: Restaurant Industry Award 2020 or Hospitality Industry (General) Award 2020. Australian Consumer Law: menu descriptions must be true.
