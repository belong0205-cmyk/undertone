# Group B electives — Asian Cookery (SITHASC020–028, SITHASC033)

## Cuisines and key flavours
| Cuisine | Key ingredients and dishes |
|---|---|
| Chinese | soy (light/dark), oyster sauce, hoisin, Shaoxing wine, ginger, spring onion, five-spice, Sichuan pepper, black bean; stir-fries, steamed fish, char siu, roast duck, dim sum, fried rice, Peking duck, mapo tofu |
| Japanese | dashi (kombu + bonito), soy, mirin, sake, miso, rice vinegar, wasabi, nori; sushi, sashimi, tempura, teriyaki, ramen, udon, soba, gyoza, katsu |
| Korean | gochujang, doenjang, gochugaru, sesame, kimchi; bibimbap, bulgogi, japchae |
| Thai | balance of hot, sour, salty and sweet: fish sauce, palm sugar, lime, tamarind, chilli, lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime, coriander root, coconut milk, shrimp paste; green/red/massaman curries, tom yum, pad Thai, som tam, larb |

| Indian, Sri Lankan | whole and ground spices (cumin, coriander, turmeric, cardamom, cinnamon, fenugreek, mustard seed), garam masala, ghee, yoghurt, tamarind, curry leaves; curries, dhal, biryani, tandoori, samosas, naan, roti, dosa, raita, chutneys |

## Basic methods (SITHASC020)
Stir-frying (very hot wok, small even pieces, cook in batches, all ingredients ready first), deep frying, steaming (bamboo baskets), braising and red-cooking, simmering, roasting (char siu), grilling (satay, yakitori), poaching (Hainanese chicken), tandoor, velveting (meat marinated in egg white and cornflour, then blanched in oil or water for a silky texture), tempering spices in hot oil or ghee (tadka). Equipment: wok, cleaver, bamboo steamer, rice cooker, mortar and pestle, clay pot, tawa, sushi mat.

## Appetisers and snacks (SITHASC021)
Spring rolls, fresh rice paper rolls, dumplings (har gow, siu mai, gyoza, wontons), samosas, pakoras, satay, prawn toast, curry puffs, tempura, karaage, Thai fish cakes, san choy bow; served with dipping sauces (nuoc cham, sweet chilli, soy-vinegar, peanut, raita).

## Stocks and soups (SITHASC022)
Dashi (infused, not boiled), Chinese chicken stock (chicken, pork bones, ginger, spring onion), pho broth (charred onion and ginger, star anise, cinnamon, cloves, beef bones, simmered long and clear), tonkotsu (pork bones boiled hard for a milky broth), tom yum, tom kha (with coconut milk), laksa, miso soup (add miso off the boil), hot and sour soup, wonton soup, congee.

## Sauces, dips and accompaniments (SITHASC023)
Nuoc cham (fish sauce, lime, sugar, water, garlic, chilli), nam jim, sweet chilli, peanut sauce, hoisin, black bean, sweet and sour, teriyaki (soy, mirin, sake, sugar), ponzu, sambal, chilli oil, raita, chutneys, pickles (kimchi, pickled ginger, pickled carrot and daikon), crispy shallots, toasted peanuts and sesame.

## Salads (SITHASC024)
Som tam (green papaya, pounded), larb (minced meat, lime, fish sauce, roasted rice powder, herbs), glass noodle salad, Vietnamese chicken salad, Thai beef salad, seaweed salad, gado gado. Balance flavours, use fresh herbs, add crunch, dress at the last minute.

## Rice and noodles (SITHASC025)
- Rice: jasmine (absorption method), basmati (rinse, soak; pilaf or biryani), sushi rice (rinse until clear, absorption, season with rice vinegar, sugar, salt), glutinous rice (soaked then steamed), fried rice from cold cooked rice, congee, coconut rice.
- Noodles: wheat (egg, hokkien, ramen, udon), rice (vermicelli, rice sticks, flat rice noodles), buckwheat (soba), mung bean glass noodles; soak or boil per type, refresh, toss in a little oil.
- Rice safety: Bacillus cereus — cool cooked rice quickly, keep at 5°C or below or 60°C or above.

## Curry pastes and powders (SITHASC026)
- Thai green paste: green chillies, lemongrass, galangal, garlic, shallots, coriander root, kaffir lime zest, shrimp paste, cumin, coriander seed (red: dried red chillies). Massaman adds dry warm spices. Malaysian rempah: shallots, garlic, chilli, galangal, lemongrass, turmeric, candlenuts, belacan. Indian: garam masala (added at the end), tandoori masala, vindaloo (chilli, vinegar, garlic), korma (nuts, cream, mild).
- Method: dry-toast whole spices, cool and grind; pound fresh aromatics or blend with a little oil; fry the paste until fragrant and the oil separates before adding liquid; store paste covered in the fridge or frozen, spices airtight away from light.

## Cooked dishes (SITHASC027)
Thai green chicken curry, beef rendang (cooked down until the coconut caramelises), butter chicken, rogan josh, dhal, beef in black bean sauce, kung pao chicken, red-braised pork belly, Vietnamese caramel pork or fish, steamed whole fish with ginger and spring onion, Hainanese chicken rice, tandoori chicken, teriyaki salmon, bulgogi, Peking duck, sweet and sour pork, nasi goreng, pad Thai.

## Desserts (SITHASC028)
Mango sticky rice, black sticky rice pudding, sago with coconut and palm sugar, red bean soup, mochi, matcha ice cream, egg tarts, sesame balls, Vietnamese che, kulfi, gulab jamun, pandan cake, banana fritters.

## Dim sum (SITHASC033)
- Served with tea (yum cha). Steamed: har gow (prawn in translucent wheat-starch dough), siu mai (pork and prawn, open top), char siu bao (yeast-raised buns), rice noodle rolls, xiao long bao (soup dumplings). Fried: spring rolls, taro puffs, sesame balls. Baked: egg tarts. Pan-fried: potstickers, turnip cake.
- Technique: even size and pleats, seal well, steam on lined baskets over rapidly boiling water for the set time, serve immediately with soy, chilli oil, vinegar or ginger.
- Food safety: pork and prawn fillings are high risk — keep chilled while folding in small batches, cook through, cool and freeze made-ahead items quickly.

## Cultural and allergen notes
Respect authentic names and methods; many Malaysian and Indonesian customers need halal food; many Indian dishes are vegetarian. Common allergens: peanuts, sesame, soy, shellfish, fish sauce, gluten in soy sauce and wheat noodles.
