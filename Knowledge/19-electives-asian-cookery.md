# Group B electives — Asian Cookery (SITHASC020–028, SITHASC033) — short notes (not in the SA Cook questionnaire)

- Key flavours: Chinese — soy, oyster sauce, ginger, spring onion, five-spice; Japanese — dashi, soy, mirin, miso; Thai — fish sauce, palm sugar, lime, chilli, lemongrass, galangal, coconut milk (hot, sour, salty, sweet in balance); Vietnamese — fresh herbs, fish sauce, lime, chilli, rice noodles and rice paper; Malaysian/Indonesian — sambal, belacan, turmeric, kecap manis; Indian — cumin, coriander, turmeric, garam masala, ghee, yoghurt.
- Methods: stir-fry (very hot wok, small even pieces, in batches, everything ready first), steam (bamboo baskets), deep fry, braise or red-cook, simmer, grill (satay), velveting (egg white and cornflour marinade), tempering spices in hot oil. Equipment: wok, cleaver, bamboo steamer, rice cooker, mortar and pestle.
- Stocks: dashi is infused, not boiled; pho broth simmers long and clear with charred onion, ginger and star anise; miso goes in off the boil.
- Rice: jasmine by absorption; sushi rice rinsed until clear and seasoned with rice vinegar, sugar and salt; fried rice from cold cooked rice. Cool cooked rice fast and keep it at 5°C or below or 60°C or above (Bacillus cereus).
- Curry pastes: toast and grind whole spices, pound or blend aromatics, fry the paste until fragrant and the oil separates; store chilled or frozen.
- Dim sum (yum cha): har gow, siu mai, char siu bao, xiao long bao steamed over rapidly boiling water; pork and prawn fillings are high risk — keep chilled, cook through.
- Allergens common in Asian food: peanuts, sesame, soy, shellfish, fish sauce, gluten in soy sauce and wheat noodles. Halal matters for many Malaysian and Indonesian customers.
