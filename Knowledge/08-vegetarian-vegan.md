# Vegetarian and vegan dishes — SITHCCC031 Prepare vegetarian and vegan dishes

## Types of vegetarian diets
| Diet | Eats | Avoids |
|---|---|---|
| Lacto-ovo vegetarian | plant foods, dairy, eggs | meat, poultry, fish, seafood |
| Lacto vegetarian | plant foods, dairy | meat, fish, eggs (common in Hindu diets) |
| Ovo vegetarian | plant foods, eggs | meat, fish, dairy |
| Vegan | only plant foods | all animal products and by-products: meat, fish, dairy, eggs, honey, gelatine, animal rennet, some wines (fining agents: isinglass, egg white, casein), bone char-refined sugar |
| Pescatarian (not vegetarian) | plant foods, dairy, eggs, fish/seafood | meat, poultry |
| Flexitarian / semi-vegetarian | mostly plant-based, occasional meat | |
| Raw food / fruitarian | uncooked plant foods / mainly fruit | cooked food |
Reasons people choose them: ethical/animal welfare, religious (Hindu, Jain, Buddhist, Seventh-day Adventist), health, environmental sustainability, taste, cost.

## Hidden animal products to check for
Gelatine (lollies, panna cotta, mousses, aspic), animal rennet in cheese (parmesan, pecorino, gorgonzola — use cheeses with microbial/vegetable rennet), chicken/beef/fish stock in soups, sauces and risotto, fish sauce, oyster sauce, shrimp paste (Asian dishes, curry pastes), anchovies (Worcestershire sauce, Caesar dressing, salsa verde), lard/suet/dripping (pastry, fried foods), egg (pasta, noodles, mayonnaise, glazes), dairy (butter, ghee, whey, casein in bread, margarine), honey, cochineal/carmine (red colouring from insects), shellac (glaze), isinglass.
Butter is dairy (made from milk), so not vegan — use vegan margarine, olive or coconut oil.
Also: fryer oil shared with meat/fish, utensils and boards used for meat (cross-contact) — vegetarians and vegans expect separate preparation. Fryer used for chicken = not vegan: tell the customer honestly, offer a pan-fried or oven-baked version or a dedicated fryer.

## Nutrition — key nutrients and plant sources
| Nutrient | Plant sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|

## Protein alternatives and how to use them
- Tofu (bean curd, from soy milk coagulated): silken (desserts, smoothies, dressings, soups), soft, firm and extra-firm (press to remove water, then marinate, fry, bake, scramble, stir-fry). Absorbs flavours.
- Tempeh: fermented whole soybeans pressed into a cake, nutty, firm — slice, steam/marinate, fry, grill.
- Seitan: wheat gluten — chewy, meat-like (not for coeliacs).
- TVP (textured vegetable protein): dried soy, rehydrate — mince substitute.
- Legumes: dried beans soaked overnight (8–12 h), drained, boiled rapidly for at least 10 minutes then simmered (red kidney beans contain lectin toxins destroyed only by proper boiling); lentils don't need soaking (red lentils cook in 15–20 min). Canned legumes: drain and rinse. Cooked legumes are PHF (Bacillus cereus, Clostridium perfringens) — cool quickly, keep ≤5°C.
- Vegan patties: chickpea + carrot + breadcrumbs + flax egg; black bean + sweet potato + oats + cumin; lentil + mushroom + walnut + chickpea flour. Binders: flax or chia egg, chickpea flour, mashed potato, oats or breadcrumbs, aquafaba; chill before cooking.
- Commercial plant-based meat alternatives, jackfruit (pulled "pork" texture), mushrooms (meaty umami), eggplant, cauliflower steaks.
- Egg replacers (vegan): aquafaba (chickpea liquid — whips like egg white for meringue/mousse), flax egg (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water), chia egg, mashed banana, apple purée, silken tofu, commercial egg replacer.
- Dairy replacers: soy/oat/almond/coconut/rice milks, coconut cream, cashew cream, vegan butter/margarine, nutritional yeast (cheesy flavour), plant-based cheeses and yoghurts.
- Umami boosters: soy sauce/tamari, miso, mushrooms (dried shiitake), tomato paste, seaweed (kombu), nutritional yeast, roasted vegetables, fermented foods.

## Typical dishes
- Vegetarian: vegetable lasagne, eggplant parmigiana, spanakopita, quiche, risotto (vegetable stock), frittata, haloumi salad, macaroni cheese, cheese soufflé, palak paneer, mushroom stroganoff.
- Vegan: dhal, chana masala, coconut vegetable curry, falafel and hummus, tofu stir-fry, grain bowls, bean chilli, ratatouille, tabbouleh, lentil "bolognese", bean or lentil burgers, vegetable sushi, gado gado (no shrimp paste), aquafaba chocolate mousse.

## Preparation and presentation principles
- Use separate, clean equipment, boards and oil; prepare before meat dishes; store vegetarian/vegan items covered and labelled above meat.
- Use vegetable stock and check every ingredient label.
- Build flavour and texture (vegetarian food must not be bland): caramelise, roast, toast spices, add acid, fresh herbs, crunch (nuts, seeds, crispy shallots), contrasting colours.
- Make dishes nutritionally balanced: include a protein source, wholegrains, vegetables, healthy fats.
- Communicate with front of house — menu labelling such as (V), (VG), (GF); never substitute ingredients without telling the customer.
