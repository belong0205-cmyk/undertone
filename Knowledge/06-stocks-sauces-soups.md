# Stocks, sauces and soups — SITHCCC029 Prepare stocks, sauces and soups

## Stocks
Definition: flavoured liquid made by gently simmering bones/vegetables/seafood with aromatics in water; base for soups, sauces, braises. Stock vs broth: stock is mainly bones, unseasoned, a base; broth is made with meat (and vegetables), seasoned and served as a light soup.
Components: the nourishing element (bones, shells, vegetables), mirepoix, bouquet garni and cold water. A bouquet garni is thyme, bay leaf, parsley stalks and peppercorns. I tie it in leek or muslin. Mirepoix is rough-cut onion, carrot and celery, about 2 : 1 : 1. That is half onion, a quarter carrot, a quarter celery. I use about 10% of the bone weight. It adds flavour, sweetness and colour. White mirepoix is for white and fish stocks. It has onion, leek, celery and mushroom trimmings, no carrot.
Uses: white stock → velouté, cream soups, braising, risotto, poaching. Brown stock → espagnole, demi-glace, jus, gravies, braises. Fish stock → fish velouté, fish soups and bisques, shallow poaching. Vegetable stock → vegetarian and vegan dishes, risotto, soups.
Hygiene when making stock: I keep bones ≤5°C until I use them. I use clean, sanitised pots and strainers. I skim the scum and fat. I never leave stock warm in the pot overnight. I strain it and cool it fast in an ice bath or shallow containers. I label it with name and date. It keeps 3–4 days in the fridge, or I freeze it.
Typical ratio: about 1 kg bones to 2–2.5 L water (bones just covered).

| Stock | Method | Simmer time |
|---|---|---|
| White chicken stock | blanch bones (optional), cover with cold water, bring to simmer, skim, add mirepoix and bouquet garni | 2–3 hours |
| White veal/beef stock | as above (veal bones blanched for clarity) | 6–8 hours |
| Brown beef/veal stock | roast bones and mirepoix until well browned, add tomato paste (pinçage), deglaze roasting tray, cover with cold water, simmer and skim | 6–8 hours |
| Fish stock (fumet) | white fish bones (non-oily: snapper, flathead, whiting; remove gills/blood), sweat with white mirepoix in butter, add white wine and cold water | 20–30 minutes (longer = bitter, cloudy) |
| Vegetable stock | vegetables and aromatics, no bones | 30–60 minutes |
| Shellfish stock | roasted/sautéed prawn, crab or crayfish shells, tomato paste, brandy | 30–45 minutes |
| Court bouillon | water + acid (vinegar, wine, lemon) + aromatics (onion, carrot, herbs, peppercorns) for poaching fish/seafood | 20–30 minutes |
| Dashi (Japanese) | kombu + bonito flakes (katsuobushi) | quick infusion |

- Rules: start with cold water (impurities coagulate and rise to be skimmed); simmer, never boil (boiling emulsifies fat and makes stock cloudy); skim regularly; never add salt (stock is reduced later); strain through a chinois/muslin without pressing; cool quickly (ice bath, shallow containers, 2h/4h cooling rule); label and refrigerate (3–4 days) or freeze (up to 3 months).
- Remouillage: second, weaker stock from bones used once. Glace: stock reduced to a syrupy glaze (glace de viande from brown stock, glace de volaille from chicken). Jus: natural roasting juices/light reduced stock.
- Faults: cloudy (boiled, not skimmed, started hot, starchy ingredients), bitter (fish stock cooked too long, burnt bones), greasy (not skimmed), weak (too little bones, too short, too much water), sour (left in danger zone → spoiled, discard).
- Convenience stocks: consistent but salty; check allergens (gluten, celery).

## Thickening agents
- Roux: equal weights of fat (butter) and flour cooked together. White roux (cooked 1–2 min, no colour — béchamel), blond roux (cooked until sandy/light straw colour — velouté), brown roux (cooked until nut brown — espagnole; less thickening power). Add cold liquid to hot roux or warm liquid to cooled roux to avoid lumps; whisk; simmer to cook out flour taste (~20 min).
- Beurre manié: kneaded raw butter and flour, whisked in at the end to adjust thickness.
- Starch slurries: cornflour (cloudy, for sweet and Chinese sauces), arrowroot (clear and glossy, for fruit glazes; breaks down if overcooked), potato starch, tapioca; mixed with cold liquid then whisked into simmering liquid.
- Liaison: egg yolks + cream; tempered and added at the end; do not boil after adding (curdles, ~82°C max).
- Reduction: evaporating liquid to thicken and concentrate flavour.
- Others: purée of vegetables (natural thickener), blood (classic coq au vin), butter (monter au beurre), rice (bisque), bread (gazpacho, romesco), gelatine (cold sauces/aspic), xanthan gum (modern, gluten-free).

## Mother (leading) sauces and derivatives
| Mother sauce | Base | Derivatives |
|---|---|---|
| Béchamel | milk + white roux, infused with onion clouté (onion studded with clove and bay) | Mornay (cheese, often yolk), Soubise (onion purée), parsley, mustard, cream, Nantua (crayfish butter/cream) |
| Velouté | white stock (chicken, veal, fish) + blond roux | Suprême (chicken velouté + cream + mushrooms), Allemande (veal velouté + liaison + lemon), Bercy (fish: shallots, white wine, parsley), white wine sauce, Normande (fish velouté, mushroom liquor, liaison), aurora (+ tomato) |
| Espagnole (brown sauce) | brown stock + brown roux + mirepoix + tomato | Demi-glace (espagnole + brown stock reduced by half) → Bordelaise (red wine, shallots, bone marrow), Chasseur/hunter (mushrooms, shallots, white wine, tomato, tarragon), Robert (onion, white wine, mustard), Madeira, Lyonnaise (onion, vinegar), Diable (cayenne, white wine), Poivrade (pepper), Bigarade (orange) |
| Tomato sauce | tomatoes, mirepoix, stock, (classically pork/bacon and roux) | Provençale, Portugaise, Creole, Bolognese-style ragù, Napoletana, arrabbiata (chilli) |
| Hollandaise (warm emulsion) | egg yolks cooked over a bain-marie with reduction (vinegar, peppercorns, shallots) or lemon, then clarified butter whisked in | Béarnaise (tarragon/shallot reduction, chervil, tarragon), Choron (Béarnaise + tomato), Foyot (Béarnaise + meat glaze), Maltaise (blood orange), Mousseline (+ whipped cream), Paloise (mint) |
- Hollandaise points: yolks cooked to a sabayon (~65–70°C) without scrambling; add warm (not hot) clarified butter slowly; hold warm (around 60°C) for a short time only — make in small batches, use pasteurised yolks, discard after 2 hours (bacteria risk). Splits if too hot (scrambles), too cold, or butter added too fast. Fix: whisk a spoonful of warm water or a new yolk in a clean bowl and slowly whisk the split sauce in.
- Clarified butter: melted butter with milk solids and water removed — higher smoke point, used for hollandaise, frying.
- Demi-glace, the classic way: equal parts espagnole and brown stock, reduced by half. I skim and strain it. Many modern kitchens reduce brown stock alone (jus) or thicken it slightly (jus lié). Reduced further it becomes a glace. Quality: deep glossy brown, clear, coats the back of a spoon. Rich meaty flavour, no fat on top, not salty, bitter or burnt.

## Contemporary and other sauces
Jus / jus lié, pan gravy, beurre blanc (shallot, wine and vinegar reduction mounted with cold butter; beurre rouge with red wine), compound butters (maître d'hôtel, garlic, café de Paris), salsas, salsa verde, pesto, chimichurri, romesco, gremolata, coulis, gastrique, sabayon; sweet: crème anglaise, chocolate, caramel, fruit coulis; Asian: soy, oyster, hoisin, sweet chilli, nuoc cham, satay, teriyaki; cold: mayonnaise family, vinaigrettes, mint, cranberry, horseradish cream, apple sauce.
Pairings: Béarnaise – steak; hollandaise – eggs Benedict, asparagus, poached fish; apple sauce – pork; mint – lamb; horseradish – roast beef; cranberry – turkey; tartare – fried fish; Mornay – cauliflower cheese; Bordelaise – beef; beurre blanc – fish.

## Sauce quality and faults
- Good sauce: correct consistency (coats the back of a spoon — nappe), smooth, glossy, well-seasoned, correct colour, no raw flour taste.
- Lumpy (liquid added too fast or temperature wrong → whisk/strain), too thick (add stock/milk), too thin (reduce or thicken), skin forming (cover with cartouche/buttered paper or dot butter), split/curdled emulsion, burnt taste (scorched — don't stir burnt bottom in; discard).
- Holding: hot sauces in bain-marie ≥60°C, covered; emulsion sauces warm briefly; cold sauces ≤5°C.

### Consommé clarification
- Clarification mix (raft): lean minced meat (or fish for fish consommé), egg whites, finely chopped mirepoix, tomato, herbs, mixed with cold well-flavoured stock.
- Bring slowly to a simmer stirring occasionally until the raft forms, then stop stirring; make a small hole in the raft; simmer gently 1–1.5 hours; ladle out through the hole and strain through muslin; remove all fat (blot with paper).
- The egg white proteins coagulate and trap impurities; the meat adds flavour.
- Garnishes: brunoise, julienne, célestine (strips of crêpe), royale (savoury custard cut in shapes), with pasta, profiteroles.
- Faults: cloudy consommé (boiled, stirred after the raft formed, greasy stock, stock not cold at start).

### Soup portion sizes and service
- Entrée 200–250 mL; main course 300–350 mL; cup ~150–200 mL.
- Serve hot soups ≥75°C at service (very hot), in warmed bowls; cold soups in chilled bowls.
- Garnish: croutons, chopped herbs, cream swirl, grated cheese, crispy bacon, oil drizzle, gremolata.
- Finishing: taste, then adjust salt, acid (lemon, vinegar) to brighten, fat (cream, butter, oil) for richness, fresh herbs at the end; check consistency.
- Store: cool rapidly (shallow containers, ice bath), refrigerate up to 3 days; reheat to 75°C once; cream/liaison added only to the portion being served.
