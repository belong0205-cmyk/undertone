## Key techniques
### Custards
- Crème anglaise: milk/cream infused with vanilla, poured onto whisked yolks and sugar (tempering), returned to low heat and stirred until it coats the back of a spoon (82–84°C, nappe); strain; cool over ice. Never boil — it curdles (scrambled egg). Fix a slightly curdled anglaise by blending immediately and straining.
- Crème pâtissière: as above but with flour/cornflour; must be boiled for 1–2 minutes to cook the starch; cover with cling film touching the surface to stop a skin; chill quickly — high-risk PHF. Variations: crème diplomat (+ whipped cream), crème mousseline (+ butter), crème chiboust (+ Italian meringue).
- Baked custards (crème caramel, brûlée): cook in a bain-marie in a low oven (~150°C) until just set with a slight wobble; overcooking causes holes/bubbles (syneresis) and weeping.
- Caramel: dry (sugar alone) or wet (sugar + water); don't stir once boiling (crystallisation) — brush sides with water; colour to amber; stop cooking by dipping pan in cold water; add hot cream carefully for caramel sauce (spits). Burns are severe — use caution.
### Meringue
- French (cold/ordinary): whites whisked to soft peaks, sugar added gradually until stiff and glossy (about 2 parts sugar to 1 part egg white by weight) — pavlova, meringue nests, folding into mousses (raw — use pasteurised whites).
- Swiss: whites and sugar warmed over a bain-marie (~50–60°C, sugar dissolved) then whisked to stiff peaks — stable; buttercream, decorations.
- Italian: hot sugar syrup (soft-ball 118–121°C) poured onto whisked whites — most stable, safe to eat without further cooking; buttercream, mousses, lemon meringue topping, macarons.
- Rules: clean grease-free bowl (fat/yolk stops whites foaming), room-temperature whites whisk to greater volume, add acid (cream of tartar/lemon) to stabilise, add sugar gradually, bake low (100–120°C) to dry. Faults: weeping/beading (sugar not dissolved, overcooked), flat (grease, underwhipped, sugar added too early).
### Gelatine and setting agents
- Gelatine (animal origin — not vegetarian/halal unless certified): leaf (soak in cold water 5 min, squeeze, melt into warm liquid) or powder (sprinkle over cold water to bloom, then melt). Never boil gelatine. Fresh pineapple, kiwi, papaya, figs and ginger contain enzymes that stop it setting — cook the fruit first or use canned. About 1 leaf ≈ 2 g; check the brand's bloom strength/strength guide.
- Agar agar (seaweed, vegan, sets firmer, must be boiled to activate, sets at room temperature).
- Others: cornflour, eggs, pectin (jams), chocolate (sets on cooling), cream cheese, freezing.
### Mousses and bavarois
Base (fruit purée, chocolate, custard) + aerating agent (whipped cream, Italian/French meringue, sabayon) + setting agent (gelatine for bavarois/fruit mousse; chocolate sets chocolate mousse). Fold gently to keep air; whip cream to soft peaks only (over-whipped cream turns grainy/butter).
### Soufflés
Hot soufflé: base (crème pâtissière or fruit purée) + whisked whites folded in; ramekins buttered and sugared with upward brush strokes; level and run thumb around the rim; bake immediately (~190–200°C, 10–15 min); serve immediately (collapses as it cools). Cold soufflé: set mousse in a ramekin with a collar to look risen.
### Frozen desserts
Ice cream base: crème anglaise (pasteurised), chilled, churned (incorporates air — overrun — and creates small crystals), hardened in freezer; sugar lowers freezing point (too much = won't freeze, too little = icy). Sorbet: sugar syrup + fruit purée; sugar density checked with a refractometer/saccharometer. Store at −18°C or below; scoop with a hot dry scoop; never refreeze melted ice cream (Listeria risk).
### Chocolate
- Types: dark (bittersweet, cocoa solids ~50–70%+), milk (milk solids added), white (cocoa butter, sugar, milk — no cocoa solids), couverture (high cocoa butter — for tempering), compound (vegetable fat — no tempering needed).
- Melt gently over a bain-marie or microwave in short bursts; no water or steam (it seizes — becomes thick and grainy); don't overheat (burns, grainy).
- Tempering (for shine, snap and shrink from moulds): dark — melt 45–50°C, cool to 27–28°C, rewarm to 31–32°C (milk 29–30°C, white 28–29°C). Methods: tabling (on marble), seeding (add chopped tempered chocolate), microwave. Test: a smear sets glossy and snaps at room temperature in a few minutes. Bloom = grey/white streaks (fat bloom from poor tempering/heat; sugar bloom from moisture).

## Dessert sauces
Crème anglaise, chocolate sauce, caramel/butterscotch, fruit coulis (puréed, sweetened, strained raspberry/mango), sabayon, Melba sauce (raspberry), syrups, honey, crème fraîche, Chantilly cream (whipped cream with sugar and vanilla).

## Plating and presentation
Contrast in temperature (hot/cold), texture (creamy/crunchy — tuiles, praline, crumbs, brandy snaps), colour and flavour (sweet, sour, bitter); correct portion size (e.g. 80–120 g ice cream/2 scoops, slice of cake ~1/12 of a 22 cm cake); height; sauce applied neatly (dots, swipes, quenelle shapes); edible garnish (mint, berries, dusting of icing sugar or cocoa, spun sugar); clean rim; cold plates for cold desserts; warm plates for hot ones; serve frozen and hot items immediately.

## Food safety for desserts
Desserts containing dairy, eggs, cream, custard and fresh fruit are PHF — store ≤5°C, cover, label and date; use pasteurised eggs for uncooked/lightly cooked egg desserts (mousse, tiramisu, French meringue); cool custards rapidly; cream-filled pastries kept chilled; allergen information for nuts, gluten, milk, egg, soy.
