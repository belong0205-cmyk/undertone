## Key techniques
### Custards
- Crème anglaise: milk/cream infused with vanilla, poured onto whisked yolks and sugar (tempering), returned to low heat and stirred until it coats the back of a spoon (82–84°C, nappe); strain; cool over ice. Never boil — it curdles (scrambled egg). Fix a slightly curdled anglaise by blending immediately and straining.
- Crème pâtissière: as above but with flour/cornflour; must be boiled for 1–2 minutes to cook the starch; cover with cling film touching the surface to stop a skin; chill quickly — high-risk PHF. Variations: crème diplomat (+ whipped cream), crème mousseline (+ butter), crème chiboust (+ Italian meringue).
- Baked custards (crème caramel, brûlée): cook in a bain-marie in a low oven (~150°C) until just set with a slight wobble; overcooking causes holes/bubbles (syneresis) and weeping. Brûlée vs caramel: brûlée = cream and yolks, baked in the serving dish, chilled, sugar top torched before service; caramel = milk/cream and whole eggs (firmer), baked over caramel in a mould, chilled and turned out so the caramel becomes a sauce. Avoid curdling: low oven ~150°C, bain-marie, strain, take out with a slight wobble.
- Lumpy custard: overheated or boiled (eggs scramble), eggs not tempered, starch not whisked in → strain and blend; next time low heat and constant stirring.
- Caramel: dry (sugar alone) or wet (sugar + water); don't stir once boiling (crystallisation) — brush sides with water; colour to amber; stop cooking by dipping pan in cold water; add hot cream carefully for caramel sauce (spits). Burns are severe — use caution.
### Meringue
- French (cold/ordinary): whites whisked to soft peaks, sugar added gradually until stiff and glossy (about 2 parts sugar to 1 part egg white by weight) — pavlova, meringue nests, folding into mousses (raw — use pasteurised whites).
- Swiss: whites and sugar warmed over a bain-marie (~50–60°C, sugar dissolved) then whisked to stiff peaks — stable; buttercream, decorations.
- Italian: hot sugar syrup (soft-ball 118–121°C) poured onto whisked whites — most stable, safe to eat without further cooking; buttercream, mousses, lemon meringue topping, macarons.
- Rules: clean grease-free bowl (fat/yolk stops whites foaming), room-temperature whites whisk to greater volume, add acid (cream of tartar/lemon) to stabilise, add sugar gradually, bake low (100–120°C) to dry. Faults: weeping/beading (sugar not dissolved, overcooked), flat (grease, underwhipped, sugar added too early).
### Gelatine and setting agents
- Gelatine (animal origin — not vegetarian/halal unless certified): leaf (soak in cold water 5 min, squeeze, melt into warm liquid) or powder (sprinkle over cold water to bloom, then melt). Never boil gelatine. Fresh pineapple, kiwi, papaya, figs and ginger contain enzymes that stop it setting — cook the fruit first or use canned. About 1 leaf ≈ 2 g; check the brand's bloom strength/strength guide.
- Agar agar (seaweed, vegan, sets firmer, must be boiled to activate, sets at room temperature).
- Pectin (from fruit, vegan; needs sugar and acid — jams, pâte de fruit). Others: cornflour, eggs, chocolate (sets on cooling), cream cheese, freezing.
### Mousses and bavarois
Base (fruit purée, chocolate, custard) + aerating agent (whipped cream, Italian/French meringue, sabayon) + setting agent (gelatine for bavarois/fruit mousse; chocolate sets chocolate mousse). Fold gently to keep air; whip cream to soft peaks only (over-whipped cream turns grainy/butter). Chocolate mousse: melt chocolate gently, fold into a yolk and sugar sabayon, fold in soft-peak cream and meringue in thirds, portion, set ≥4 h at ≤5°C; pasteurised eggs. Runny mousse: too little chocolate or gelatine, gelatine not dissolved, cream over- or under-whipped, mix too warm, overfolded, not chilled long enough.
### Soufflés
Hot soufflé: base (crème pâtissière or fruit purée) + whisked whites folded in; ramekins buttered and sugared with upward brush strokes; level and run thumb around the rim; bake immediately (~190–200°C, 10–15 min); serve immediately (collapses as it cools). Rises because air in the whites expands and water turns to steam while the egg sets. Collapses: whites under- or over-whipped, overfolded, oven too cool or door opened, underbaked, not served at once. Cold soufflé: set mousse in a ramekin with a collar to look risen.
### Frozen desserts
Ice cream base: crème anglaise (pasteurised), chilled, churned (incorporates air — overrun — and creates small crystals), hardened in freezer; sugar lowers freezing point (too much = won't freeze, too little = icy). Sorbet: sugar syrup + fruit purée; sugar density checked with a refractometer/saccharometer. Store at −18°C or below; scoop with a hot dry scoop; never refreeze melted ice cream (Listeria risk).
### Chocolate
- Types: dark (bittersweet, cocoa solids ~50–70%+), milk (milk solids added), white (cocoa butter, sugar, milk — no cocoa solids), couverture (high cocoa butter — for tempering), compound (vegetable fat — no tempering needed).
- Melt gently over a bain-marie or microwave in short bursts; no water or steam (it seizes — becomes thick and grainy); don't overheat (burns, grainy).
- Tempering (for shine, snap and shrink from moulds): dark — melt 45–50°C, cool to 27–28°C, rewarm to 31–32°C (milk 29–30°C, white 28–29°C). Methods: tabling (on marble), seeding (add chopped tempered chocolate), microwave. Test: a smear sets glossy and snaps at room temperature in a few minutes. Bloom = grey/white streaks (fat bloom from poor tempering/heat; sugar bloom from moisture).
### Ganache
- Definition: a smooth emulsion of chocolate and hot cream (cream 35% fat), sometimes with butter, glucose/invert sugar, liqueur or flavourings. Used as a sauce, glaze/drip, cake filling or frosting (whipped ganache), tart filling, macaron filling and truffle centres.
- Ratio = chocolate : cream by weight. More chocolate → firmer. Milk and white chocolate contain less cocoa solids and more sugar/milk, so they set softer and need more chocolate than dark for the same firmness. Typical starting ratios (adjust to the brand and the standard recipe):

| Use | Dark (55–70%) | Milk | White |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sauce (pouring, served warm) | 1 : 2 | 1 : 1 | 1 : 1 to 1.5 : 1 |
| Glaze, drip, soft filling, whipped ganache | 1 : 1 | 2 : 1 | 3 : 1 |
| Firm: truffles, piping, set tart filling | 2 : 1 | 2.5 : 1 to 3 : 1 | 3 : 1 to 4 : 1 |

  Example: dark truffles 200 g chocolate + 100 g cream; white chocolate drip 300 g chocolate + 100 g cream.
- Method: chop chocolate finely; heat cream until just simmering (about 80–85°C, do not boil hard); pour over the chocolate, rest 1–2 minutes, then stir gently from the centre outwards until smooth and glossy (or use a stick blender, blade under the surface, to avoid air bubbles); add soft butter at about 35–40°C for extra shine and smoothness. Use warm (about 32–35°C) for glazing; cool and set for piping/truffles; chill then whip for whipped ganache.
- Truffles: firm ganache set in the fridge, piped or scooped, rolled, then coated in cocoa powder, chopped nuts or tempered chocolate.
- Faults: split/oily or grainy (too hot, too much fat, overheated chocolate or stirred too hard) — fix by blending in a little warm cream or milk with a stick blender; too soft (not enough chocolate) — add melted chocolate; too firm — add warm cream; seized (water got in) — chocolate can't be tempered, use it in a baked item.
- Storage: contains cream, so cover and refrigerate (≤5°C), label and date; use within the recipe's shelf life (usually a few days to 2 weeks); bring to room temperature before use.

## Dessert sauces
Crème anglaise, chocolate, caramel/butterscotch, fruit coulis, sabayon, Melba (raspberry), syrups, Chantilly cream.

## Plating and presentation
Contrast of temperature, texture (tuiles, praline, crumbs), colour and flavour; correct portion (e.g. 2 scoops ice cream, 1/12 of a 22 cm cake); height; neat sauce (dots, swipes, quenelles); edible garnish; clean rim; cold plates for cold desserts, warm for hot; serve frozen and hot items at once.

## Food safety for desserts
Desserts containing dairy, eggs, cream, custard and fresh fruit are PHF — store ≤5°C, cover, label and date; use pasteurised eggs for uncooked/lightly cooked egg desserts (mousse, tiramisu, French meringue); cool custards rapidly; cream-filled pastries kept chilled; allergen information for nuts, gluten, milk, egg, soy.
Gluten-free desserts: GF flours (rice, almond meal, cornflour, GF blend + xanthan), clean equipment and separate sieves, check labels (biscuit crumbs, malt, oats), call it GF only when it has no gluten. Examples: pavlova, flourless chocolate cake, panna cotta, crème brûlée, sorbet, orange and almond cake.
