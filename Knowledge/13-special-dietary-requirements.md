# Special dietary requirements — SITHCCC042 Prepare food to meet special dietary requirements

## Why customers have special requirements
- Health/medical: food allergies and intolerances, coeliac disease, diabetes, heart disease (low fat). Also hypertension (low salt), kidney disease, weight management, dysphagia (texture modified), elderly, children. Recovery (high energy/protein), IBS (low FODMAP), pregnancy (Listeria-risk foods, high-mercury fish, alcohol).
- Lifestyle/ethical: vegetarian, vegan, paleo, keto (low carb), organic, raw food.
- Cultural/religious: halal, kosher, Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, Seventh-day Adventist, fasting periods (Ramadan, Lent).

## Allergy vs intolerance
- Food allergy: immune system reaction (IgE) to a food protein; even a trace amount can cause a reaction within minutes — hives, swelling of lips/face/eyes, vomiting, abdominal pain, difficulty breathing, wheeze, persistent cough, dizziness/collapse → anaphylaxis can be fatal. Response: call 000, use adrenaline autoinjector (EpiPen/Anapen) in the outer thigh, lay flat (or sit if breathing difficult — don't stand/walk), note the time, second dose after 5 minutes if no response, CPR if needed. Report the incident.
- Food intolerance: non-immune reaction (digestive), usually dose-related and slower; unpleasant but not life-threatening. Examples: lactose intolerance (lack of lactase enzyme), fructose, histamine, food additives (MSG, sulphites, colours), FODMAPs.
- Preference: personal choice (likes, dislikes, lifestyle) — no medical reaction, but respect it.
- Cross-contamination = moving bacteria or other hazards (raw chicken juice onto salad); cross-contact = moving an allergen protein into an allergen-free food (same knife for satay then salad) — cooking does not remove allergens.
- Coeliac disease: autoimmune disease — gluten damages the small intestine lining; requires strict lifelong gluten-free diet (no trace contamination). Gluten is in wheat (including spelt, durum, semolina, couscous, burghul), rye, barley (malt), oats (oats contain avenin; not acceptable in Australian "gluten-free" labelling), triticale. Gluten-free in Australia means no detectable gluten.
- Hidden gluten: soy sauce (use tamari), stock powders, gravies, roux-thickened sauces, batters and crumbs, beer, malt vinegar, sausages, processed meats, imitation crab, some spice mixes, communal fryer oil.

## Common allergens and what to watch
The 11 usually listed: peanut, tree nuts, milk, egg, sesame, soy, fish, crustacea, mollusc, wheat (gluten), lupin; PEAL also requires barley, oats and rye (gluten) and added sulphites ≥10 mg/kg to be declared; the cereal is named in bold and "gluten" goes in the "Contains" statement.
| Allergen | Hidden sources / notes |
|---|---|
| Peanut | satay, Asian sauces, peanut oil, praline, desserts, crushed nuts as garnish |
| Tree nuts (almond, cashew, hazelnut, walnut, pecan, pistachio, macadamia, Brazil, pine nut) | pesto (pine nuts), marzipan, frangipane, nougat, praline, nut oils and milks, Nutella, some curries (cashew paste) |
| Milk (dairy) | butter, cream, cheese, whey, casein, ghee, milk powder in bread, sauces, mashed potato |
| Egg | mayonnaise, aioli, pasta, batters, glazes (egg wash), meringue, cakes, some breads |
| Sesame | tahini, hummus, sesame oil, seeds on buns, dukkah, Asian dressings |
| Soy | soy sauce, tofu, edamame, miso, soy lecithin in chocolate, processed meats |
| Fish | fish sauce, Worcestershire (anchovy), Caesar dressing, stocks |
| Crustacea | prawns, crab, lobster; shrimp paste, prawn crackers, XO sauce, bisque |
| Molluscs | oysters, mussels, squid, octopus, scallops; oyster sauce |
| Wheat / gluten | see above |
| Lupin | lupin flour in some breads, pastas, gluten-free products |
| Sulphites | wine, dried fruit (apricots), sausages, vinegar, preserved prawns |

## Preventing allergen cross-contact (step by step)
1. Take the order accurately; front of house confirms the allergy with the customer and alerts the kitchen (docket marked "ALLERGY").
2. Check the standard recipe and the labels of all ingredients (including sauces, garnishes, oils); use the allergen matrix.
3. Clean and sanitise the bench and equipment before starting; wash hands and put on fresh gloves.
4. Use separate/colour-coded (often purple) equipment, boards, utensils, pans and fresh oil; don't share fryers.
5. Prepare the allergen-free meal first or in a separate area; cover it; label it.
6. Don't substitute ingredients without checking; if a substitution is made, inform the customer.
7. Keep the dish separate on the pass; one person (often the chef) carries it/communicates to the server; serve directly to the correct customer.
8. If unsure, say so honestly — never guess. If the business cannot guarantee it, inform the customer.
- High-risk points in service: taking the order, docket entry, substitutions and garnishes, shared fryers and grills, plating at the pass, delivery to the wrong table.
- Menu and allergen information: every ingredient including sauces and garnishes, allergen matrix, symbols, "please tell staff about allergies", and "we can't guarantee allergen-free" where true.
- Label reading: ingredient list with allergens in bold, "Contains" and "May contain" statements, other names (casein, whey = milk; albumin = egg; semolina, spelt = wheat; tahini = sesame); recheck when a brand or supplier changes.
- Allergen incident: first aid and 000, tell the manager, write an incident report (customer, dish, ingredients and labels, time, symptoms, actions, staff), keep the dish and packaging, find the cause, retrain, update procedures.
- Nut-free service: I check every label and "may contain" warning. Hidden nuts: pesto, praline, marzipan, satay, nut oils and milks. I store nuts sealed and apart. I use dedicated boards and utensils and clean and sanitise before prep. No nut garnishes near the pass. The allergen matrix is up to date. I brief the floor staff, and the chef confirms the plate.
- "I'm allergic to gluten" but orders soy sauce. I explain politely that regular soy sauce contains wheat. I offer tamari (the label must say gluten-free) or leave it out. I ask whether it is coeliac disease or a preference. I confirm the change, mark the docket and tell the chef. I never serve it quietly.

## Diet types and how to adapt dishes
- Gluten-free: I replace flour with rice flour, cornflour, potato starch or tapioca. Buckwheat, chickpea (besan) flour and GF blends also work. I use GF pasta and bread, tamari, polenta, rice and quinoa. I thicken sauces with cornflour, arrowroot or reduction. GF in a shared kitchen: the docket is marked. I check every label (stock, sauces, crumbs). I prep before any flour is used, because flour dust floats. I use a clean bench and clean hands. I use dedicated purple boards, utensils, sieves, toaster and fryer. I cook GF pasta in fresh water. I plate it separately, covered and labelled. The chef confirms at the pass.
- Dairy-free/lactose-free: plant milks, coconut cream, dairy-free spreads, oil; lactose-free milk (lactose intolerance only — not suitable for milk allergy).
- Egg-free: aquafaba, flax egg, commercial egg replacer; egg-free pasta and mayonnaise.
- Diabetic: controlled carbohydrate portions, low GI foods (wholegrains, legumes), little added sugar. Also high fibre and regular meals. Low fat or heart-healthy: lean meat, no skin, unsaturated oils. No cream or butter sauces. I grill, steam or poach instead of frying. Low salt: I use herbs, spices, citrus and garlic for flavour. No stock powders, soy sauce or processed meats. High fibre: wholegrains, legumes, vegetables, fruit with skin. High protein or energy: I enrich with milk powder, eggs, cream and nut butters. I serve small frequent meals.
- Low FODMAP (IBS): limit onion, garlic, wheat, apple, pear, stone fruit, legumes, lactose. I use garlic-infused oil and green spring onion tops. Texture-modified (dysphagia — IDDSI levels 3–7 food, 0–4 drinks): follow the speech pathologist exactly.
- Pregnancy: no deli meats, pâté, soft cheeses (unless cooked), raw seafood, smoked salmon. Also no pre-cut salads, raw eggs, undercooked meat or alcohol. Limit high-mercury fish (flake, swordfish, marlin).

## Cultural and religious requirements
| Group | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Islam (halal) | permitted foods only; meat from animals slaughtered by a Muslim according to Islamic rites (certified halal); no pork or pork products (lard, gelatine from pork, bacon), no alcohol (including in cooking — wine, beer batter, vanilla extract with alcohol), no blood. Haram = forbidden. Fasting during Ramadan (sunrise to sunset) |
| Judaism (kosher) | only kosher-certified meat from animals with split hooves that chew the cud (beef, lamb — not pork); no shellfish or fish without fins and scales; meat and dairy never cooked, served or eaten together (separate equipment); slaughter by a shochet; Passover — no leavened products |
| Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism | Hindu: many vegetarian, no beef (cow is sacred), fasting days. Buddhist: many vegetarian or vegan, some avoid onion, garlic, alcohol. Jain: strict vegetarian, no root vegetables, eggs or honey |
| Sikh, Seventh-day Adventist, Christian | Sikh: no halal/kosher meat, many vegetarian. Adventist: often vegetarian, no pork or shellfish. Some Christians: no meat on Lent Fridays |

## Australian Dietary Guidelines (basis of healthy menus)
Five food groups: 1 vegetables and legumes; 2 fruit; 3 grain foods, mostly wholegrain. 4 lean meat, poultry, fish, eggs, tofu, nuts, seeds, legumes. 5 milk, yoghurt, cheese and alternatives. I limit saturated fat, salt, added sugar and alcohol. Macronutrients: carbohydrate gives energy; protein builds and repairs. Fat gives energy and vitamins A, D, E, K. Micronutrients are vitamins and minerals. We also need fibre and water. Five sources — protein: meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, tofu, nuts. Carbohydrate: bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, oats, legumes, fruit. Fats: olive oil, butter, avocado, nuts and seeds, oily fish, cheese.

## Communicating with customers
Listen and confirm the need, explain ingredients accurately, offer alternatives, never promise what the kitchen can't guarantee, write it clearly on the docket, communicate front to back of house. Menu symbols: V, VG/VE, GF, DF, H; allergen information available on request.
