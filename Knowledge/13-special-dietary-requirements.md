# Special dietary requirements — SITHCCC042 Prepare food to meet special dietary requirements

## Why customers have special requirements
- Health/medical: food allergies, intolerances, coeliac disease, diabetes, heart disease (low fat, low cholesterol), hypertension (low sodium), kidney disease (low protein/potassium/phosphorus), weight management, dysphagia (swallowing difficulty — texture modified), pregnancy (avoid Listeria-risk foods, raw/undercooked foods, high-mercury fish, alcohol), elderly, infants and children, illness recovery (high energy/protein), food-related conditions like IBS (low FODMAP).
- Lifestyle/ethical: vegetarian, vegan, paleo, keto (low carb), organic, raw food.
- Cultural/religious: halal, kosher, Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, Seventh-day Adventist, fasting periods (Ramadan, Lent).

## Allergy vs intolerance
- Food allergy: immune system reaction (IgE) to a food protein; even a trace amount can cause a reaction within minutes — hives, swelling of lips/face/eyes, vomiting, abdominal pain, difficulty breathing, wheeze, persistent cough, dizziness/collapse → anaphylaxis can be fatal. Response: call 000, use adrenaline autoinjector (EpiPen/Anapen) in the outer thigh, lay flat (or sit if breathing difficult — don't stand/walk), note the time, second dose after 5 minutes if no response, CPR if needed. Report the incident.
- Food intolerance: non-immune reaction (digestive), usually dose-related and slower; unpleasant but not life-threatening. Examples: lactose intolerance (lack of lactase enzyme), fructose, histamine, food additives (MSG, sulphites, colours), FODMAPs.
- Coeliac disease: autoimmune disease — gluten damages the small intestine lining; requires strict lifelong gluten-free diet (no trace contamination). Gluten is in wheat (including spelt, durum, semolina, couscous, burghul), rye, barley (malt), oats (oats contain avenin; not acceptable in Australian "gluten-free" labelling), triticale. Gluten-free in Australia means no detectable gluten.
- Hidden gluten: soy sauce (use tamari), stock powders, gravies, roux-thickened sauces, batters and crumbs, beer, malt vinegar, sausages, processed meats, imitation crab, some spice mixes, communal fryer oil.

## Preventing allergen cross-contact (step by step)
1. Take the order accurately; front of house confirms the allergy with the customer and alerts the kitchen (docket marked "ALLERGY").
2. Check the standard recipe and the labels of all ingredients (including sauces, garnishes, oils); use the allergen matrix.
3. Clean and sanitise the bench and equipment before starting; wash hands and put on fresh gloves.
4. Use separate/colour-coded (often purple) equipment, boards, utensils, pans and fresh oil; don't share fryers.
5. Prepare the allergen-free meal first or in a separate area; cover it; label it.
6. Don't substitute ingredients without checking; if a substitution is made, inform the customer.
7. Keep the dish separate on the pass; one person (often the chef) carries it/communicates to the server; serve directly to the correct customer.
8. If unsure, say so honestly — never guess. If the business cannot guarantee it, inform the customer.

## Diet types and how to adapt dishes
- Gluten-free: replace flour with rice flour, cornflour, potato starch, tapioca, buckwheat, chickpea (besan) flour, GF blends; use GF pasta/bread, tamari, polenta, rice, quinoa; thicken sauces with cornflour/arrowroot or reduction; separate fryer.
- Dairy-free/lactose-free: plant milks, coconut cream, dairy-free spreads, oil; lactose-free milk (lactose intolerance only — not suitable for milk allergy).
- Egg-free: aquafaba, flax egg, commercial egg replacer; egg-free pasta and mayonnaise.
- Diabetic (diabetes type 1 and 2): control carbohydrate portions, low GI carbohydrates (wholegrains, legumes), limit added sugar, high fibre, healthy fats, regular meals; artificial sweeteners in desserts; avoid sugary sauces.
- Low-fat / heart-healthy: lean meat, remove skin from poultry, grill/steam/poach/bake instead of frying, unsaturated oils, low-fat dairy, avoid cream and butter sauces, more vegetables and wholegrains.
- Low sodium (salt): cook from scratch, use herbs, spices, citrus, garlic, vinegar for flavour; avoid stock powders, soy sauce, processed meats, cheese, salty snacks.
- High fibre: wholegrains, legumes, vegetables, fruit with skin, nuts and seeds.
- High protein/high energy (recovery, athletes, elderly): enrich foods with milk powder, eggs, cream, nut butters, small frequent meals.
- Low FODMAP (IBS): limit onion, garlic, wheat, some fruits (apple, pear, stone fruit), legumes, lactose, sweeteners — use garlic-infused oil, green parts of spring onion.
- Texture-modified (dysphagia — IDDSI framework): food levels 3 liquidised, 4 puréed, 5 minced and moist, 6 soft and bite-sized, 7 regular (and easy to chew); drink levels 0 thin to 4 extremely thick. Follow speech pathologist instructions exactly; avoid mixed consistencies and hard/stringy bits.
- Pregnancy: avoid deli meats, pâté, soft cheeses (brie, camembert, ricotta unless cooked), raw seafood, smoked salmon, pre-cut salads and fruit, raw eggs, undercooked meat; limit high-mercury fish (shark/flake, swordfish, marlin); no alcohol.

## Cultural and religious requirements
| Group | Requirements |
|---|---|
| Islam (halal) | permitted foods only; meat from animals slaughtered by a Muslim according to Islamic rites (certified halal); no pork or pork products (lard, gelatine from pork, bacon), no alcohol (including in cooking — wine, beer batter, vanilla extract with alcohol), no blood. Haram = forbidden. Fasting during Ramadan (sunrise to sunset) |
| Judaism (kosher) | only kosher-certified meat from animals with split hooves that chew the cud (beef, lamb — not pork); no shellfish or fish without fins and scales; meat and dairy never cooked, served or eaten together (separate equipment); slaughter by a shochet; Passover — no leavened products |
| Hinduism | many are vegetarian; no beef (cow is sacred); some avoid onion, garlic, alcohol; fasting days |
| Buddhism | many vegetarian or vegan; some avoid onion/garlic and alcohol |
| Jainism | strict vegetarian, no root vegetables (onion, garlic, potato, carrot), no eggs, no honey |
| Sikhism | no halal/kosher (ritually slaughtered) meat; many vegetarian; no alcohol/tobacco |
| Seventh-day Adventist | often vegetarian, no pork/shellfish, no alcohol, caffeine avoided |
| Christianity | some avoid meat on Fridays in Lent (fish allowed), Orthodox fasting periods (vegan) |

## Australian Dietary Guidelines (basis of healthy menus)
Five food groups: vegetables and legumes; fruit; grain (cereal) foods, mostly wholegrain; lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, tofu, nuts and seeds, legumes; milk, yoghurt, cheese and alternatives (mostly reduced fat). Limit saturated fat, added salt, added sugars and alcohol; drink water. Macronutrients: carbohydrates (energy), proteins (growth and repair), fats (energy, fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K); micronutrients: vitamins and minerals; plus fibre and water.

## Communicating with customers
Listen and confirm the requirement, explain menu options and ingredients accurately, offer suitable alternatives, don't promise what the kitchen can't guarantee, record the request clearly on the docket, communicate between front and back of house, and follow up after service. Menus may show symbols such as V (vegetarian), VG/VE (vegan), GF (gluten-free), DF (dairy-free), H (halal); allergen information must be available on request.
