# Cakes, pastries and breads — SITHCCC041 Produce cakes, pastries and breads (also SITHPAT014 Produce yeast-based bakery products)

## Ingredients and their functions
| Ingredient | Function |
|---|---|
| Flour | structure (gluten from wheat proteins glutenin + gliadin when mixed with water). Bread/strong/baker's flour: high protein 11–14% (strong gluten, chewy). Plain/all-purpose: ~10%. Cake/soft flour: low protein (~8%) for tender cakes. Self-raising: plain flour + baking powder. '00' for pasta/pizza. Wholemeal, rye, spelt, gluten-free blends |
| Sugar | sweetness, tenderness, moisture (hygroscopic), browning/caramelisation, aeration in creaming, feeds yeast, stabilises meringue, preserves |
| Fat (butter, margarine, shortening, oil, lard) | tenderness ("shortens" gluten strands), flavour, moisture, aeration (creaming), flakiness (lamination in puff pastry), keeping quality |
| Eggs | structure (proteins set), aeration (foam), emulsifying (yolk lecithin), binding, colour, flavour, moisture, glaze (egg wash) |
| Liquid (milk, water) | hydrates flour/forms gluten, dissolves sugar and salt, activates raising agents, creates steam |
| Raising agents | Chemical: baking powder (bicarbonate of soda + acid, e.g. cream of tartar; double-acting), bicarbonate of soda (needs an acid such as buttermilk, yoghurt, golden syrup, cocoa). Biological: yeast (fermentation → CO2 + alcohol). Mechanical/physical: air (creaming, whisking, sifting, folding, lamination) and steam (choux, puff pastry, Yorkshire pudding) |
| Salt | flavour, strengthens gluten, controls yeast activity (too much kills/slows yeast; don't put in direct contact) |
| Flavourings | vanilla, spices, zest, cocoa, chocolate, nuts, dried fruit |

## Cake-making methods
| Method | How | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Creaming | cream butter and sugar until light and fluffy (traps air), add eggs gradually (prevents curdling — add a little flour if it starts to split), fold in sifted flour alternately with liquid | butter cake, Victoria sponge (sandwich), cupcakes, Madeira, fruit cake, pound cake |
| Whisking / foaming | whisk eggs and sugar to ribbon stage (sabayon), carefully fold in sifted flour (and melted butter for Genoise) | Genoise, sponge, Swiss roll, Savoiardi/ladyfingers, angel food cake (whites only) |
| Rubbing-in | rub cold fat into flour with fingertips to breadcrumb stage, then add liquid | scones, shortcrust pastry, crumble, rock cakes |
| Melting | melt fat, sugar and syrup together, cool slightly, mix into dry ingredients | gingerbread, brownies, Anzac biscuits, some muffins |
| All-in-one / blending | all ingredients beaten together (usually with extra baking powder/soft margarine) | quick cakes, commercial batters |
| Muffin method | mix wet and dry separately, combine briefly (lumpy batter — overmixing = tough, tunnels) | muffins, pancakes |
- Sponge types: Genoise (whisked whole eggs, melted butter — fine, moist), Victoria (creamed butter — denser, sandwich), fatless sponge (whisked eggs and sugar — very light, Swiss roll), chiffon (oil and whipped whites — moist, tall), angel food (whites only — white, airy).
- Baking: preheat oven (cakes usually 160–180°C), prepare tins (grease, line, flour), fill to about two-thirds, centre of the oven, don't open the door early (collapse).
- Test if cooked: skewer comes out clean, springs back when lightly pressed, shrinks from the sides of the tin, golden colour. Cool in the tin ~5–10 min then on a wire rack.
- Faults: sunken centre (underbaked, too much raising agent/sugar, door opened early, oven too cool), peaked/cracked top (oven too hot, too much flour), heavy/dense (too little raising agent, mixture curdled, overmixed after flour, under-creamed), tunnels (overmixed), dry (overbaked, too little fat/liquid), fruit sunk (batter too thin, fruit wet — toss in flour), burnt outside raw inside (oven too hot).
- Finishes: buttercream (butter + icing sugar; Swiss/Italian meringue buttercream), glacé icing (icing sugar + water/juice), royal icing (egg white + icing sugar — hard set for decorations), fondant (rolled sugar paste), ganache (chocolate + cream; dark chocolate 1:1 for glazing/filling, 2:1 for truffles — milk and white ratios in the desserts notes, Ganache), cream cheese frosting, glazes, piping, crumb coating, mirror glaze.

## Pastry
| Pastry | Ratio / method | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| Shortcrust (pâte brisée) | half fat to flour (e.g. 200 g flour : 100 g butter) + cold water; rubbing-in; minimal handling; rest/chill before rolling | quiche, pies, tarts, pasties |
| Sweet pastry (pâte sucrée / sablée) | flour, butter, sugar, eggs; creamed or rubbed-in; rich, crisp | fruit tarts, lemon tart, tartlets |
| Puff pastry (feuilletage) | I fold and roll dough (détrempe) around a butter slab. I repeat this many times (lamination). This makes hundreds of layers, and steam between them lifts the pastry. I keep the butter and dough cold and equally firm (about 4°C). I rest it 20–30 min chilled between turns. Turns are single (fold in three) or double (book). Six single turns give about 729 layers. Classic puff gives the most even layers and the highest rise. French method: butter block wrapped like an envelope. English method: butter on two-thirds, folded in thirds. Rough (Scotch) puff mixes cold butter cubes into the dough, then folds. It is quicker, with fewer layers and a less even rise. It suits sausage rolls and pie tops. | vol-au-vents, beef Wellington, sausage rolls, mille-feuille, palmiers, pie tops |
| Choux pastry (pâte à choux) | water/milk + butter boiled, flour added all at once and cooked (panada) until it leaves the sides of the pan, cooled slightly, eggs beaten in gradually to a piping consistency; baked at high heat (~200–220°C) then lowered — rises by steam, hollow | profiteroles, éclairs, croquembouche, gougères, Paris-Brest, churros |
| Filo (phyllo) | paper-thin sheets (usually bought), brushed with melted butter/oil, kept covered with a damp cloth so it doesn't dry out | baklava, spanakopita, strudel, samosas |
| Others | hot water crust (lard, moulded warm — pork pies), suet (steamed puddings), flaky (partly laminated), strudel (stretched paper-thin) | |
- Frangipane (almond cream): butter and sugar creamed, eggs, almond meal (a little flour); spread in tart cases under fruit (pear and almond tart, Bakewell), pithivier; baked until set and golden; tree-nut allergen.
- Choux rises because the water turns to steam. The egg and cooked starch set around the hollow. Five failures and fixes. 1 collapsed — underbaked or the door was opened. Fix: bake until dry and deep golden, keep the door shut. 2 heavy, not hollow — too much egg or the panada was undercooked. Fix: add egg little by little, cook the panada until it leaves the pan. 3 soggy — not dried out or filled too early. Fix: pierce it, return it to a low oven, fill close to service. 4 flat — mixture too soft or oven too cool. Fix: piping consistency, start at 200–220°C. 5 cracked — piped unevenly. Fix: pipe evenly, lower the heat once risen.
- Quiche custard: about 1 egg per 100–125 mL cream or milk, salt, pepper, nutmeg. Controls: blind-bake and seal the case (egg wash), filling first then custard, bake ~150–160°C until just set with a slight wobble (overbaked = curdled, weeping); PHF — ≤5°C.
- Rules for short pastry: keep everything cold, don't overwork (gluten → tough, shrinks), rest in fridge (relaxes gluten, firms fat), roll evenly in one direction, blind bake (line with paper, fill with baking beans/rice, bake, remove weights, dry out) for wet fillings to avoid soggy bottoms; egg wash for colour/seal.
- Pastry faults: tough (overhandled, too much water, too little fat). Shrinking (not rested, stretched when lining the tin). Crumbly (too little water, too much fat). Soggy base (not blind baked, oven too cool). Puff not rising: butter melted into the dough, not rested, oven too cool. Or the edges were sealed with egg wash.

## Breads and yeast products
- Yeast types: fresh (compressed), dried active, instant dried. I use about twice as much fresh as dried. I rehydrate dried active in warm water. Instant goes straight into the flour. Fermentation: yeast feeds on sugars and makes CO₂, alcohol and acids. The gas lifts the dough and opens the crumb. The alcohol and acids add flavour. The gluten stretches to hold the gas. Proving depends on temperature: ideal 26–30°C, cold slows it, above about 55°C kills it. Time, and the amount and freshness of yeast, matter too. Sugar: a little feeds yeast, a lot slows it. Salt controls yeast; too much kills it, so I keep them apart. Flour strength, hydration, and fat and eggs (they slow it) also count.
- Gluten develops by kneading (stretchy, elastic, smooth dough; windowpane test — dough stretches thin without tearing).
- Bread-making stages: 1) weigh/scale ingredients accurately; 2) mix; 3) knead (10–15 min by hand or 5–10 min on a mixer with dough hook); 4) bulk fermentation/first prove (covered, warm, until doubled ~1 h); 5) knock back/degas (redistributes yeast, even texture); 6) divide and scale; 7) round/pre-shape, rest (bench rest); 8) shape/mould; 9) final prove (in a prover ~30–40°C with humidity until nearly doubled; finger-poke test springs back slowly); 10) finish (egg wash, slash/score, seeds, flour dust); 11) bake (bread usually 200–230°C; steam in the first minutes for a crisp crust; oven spring); 12) cool on a wire rack.
- Test if bread is cooked: hollow sound when tapped underneath, golden crust, core ~93–96°C.
- Dough methods: straight dough (all ingredients mixed at once), sponge and dough / pre-ferments (poolish, biga — part of the flour/water/yeast fermented first for flavour), sourdough (natural leaven/starter of wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria), no-knead (long slow fermentation), enriched doughs (with butter, eggs, sugar, milk — brioche, challah, bun dough, doughnuts), laminated yeast doughs (croissant, Danish — yeast dough + butter layers).
- Products: loaves, rolls, baguette, ciabatta, focaccia, sourdough, rye, pizza, pita, naan, flatbreads. Also brioche, hot cross buns, doughnuts, croissants, Danish, bagels (boiled then baked).
- Bread faults: dense/heavy (underproved, too little yeast, old/dead yeast, flour too weak, too much salt), collapsed/large holes or sour (overproved), pale crust (oven too cool, not enough sugar), cracked/burst sides (underproved, oven too hot), close texture (insufficient kneading).
- Baguette: lean dough (flour, water, salt, yeast), long and thin, crisp golden crust, open irregular crumb, slashed, baked with steam. Ciabatta: very wet dough, big holes. Sourdough: natural starter, tangy, chewy. Brioche: butter and eggs, soft, rich. Focaccia: olive oil, dimpled.
- Quick breads (no yeast): scones, soda bread, damper, muffins, banana bread. Scones: rubbing-in, light handling, cut straight down, bake hot ~220°C. Biscuits and slices: creaming (shortbread), melting (Anzac), rubbing-in; store airtight, label allergens.

## Storage and presentation
Cool completely before filling, icing or storing. Store cakes airtight at room temperature unless they contain cream, custard, cream cheese or fresh fruit (refrigerate ≤5°C). Freeze unfilled sponges, pastries and doughs well-wrapped. Label with name, date and allergens (gluten, egg, milk, nuts, sesame); custard and cream fillings are PHF — cool fast, ≤5°C; separate equipment and no flour dust for gluten-free items. Present with neat even slices (hot clean knife), consistent portions (cake marker), appropriate garnish and sauces.
