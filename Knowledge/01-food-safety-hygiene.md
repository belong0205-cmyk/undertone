# Food safety — SITXFSA005 Use hygienic practices for food safety, SITXFSA006 Participate in safe food handling practices

## Key definitions
- Food safety program (FSP): documented system that identifies hazards, controls them and keeps records; based on HACCP. Required for food service to vulnerable persons (Std 3.3.1) and where state law requires (Std 3.2.1).
- Potentially hazardous food (PHF): food that must be kept at ≤5°C or ≥60°C to stop pathogens growing or toxins forming. Examples: raw and cooked meat, poultry, seafood, dairy, eggs and egg products, cooked rice and pasta, cooked vegetables and legumes, prepared salads and cut fruit, sandwiches, soups, sauces, gravies, foods containing these. (3.2.2 cl 1)
- Ready-to-eat (RTE) food: eaten without further cooking (salads, sliced cold meats, desserts, sushi).
- Contamination means something harmful or unwanted is in the food. Cross-contamination means a hazard moves from one food, surface or person to another. Examples: raw chicken juice drips onto salad below it. One board or knife is used for raw meat and then for salad. Dirty hands or a dirty cloth touch ready-to-eat food.
- Food handler: anyone who handles food or surfaces that touch food. Food safety supervisor (FSS): certified person who supervises and advises food handlers.
- Use-by date: food must not be sold or eaten after it (safety). Best-before date: food is still safe after it but quality may drop; it can be sold if not damaged/deteriorated.

## Food safety hazards (4 types)
- Biological: bacteria, viruses, parasites, moulds/yeasts, toxins (e.g. Salmonella, Listeria, norovirus).
- Chemical: cleaning chemicals, pesticides, sanitiser residue, pest baits, heavy metals, natural toxins (e.g. green potatoes – solanine; red kidney beans – lectin if undercooked; ciguatoxin; histamine/scombroid).
- Physical: glass, metal, hair, jewellery, bandages, plastic, bone, stones, pests and droppings, fingernails.
- Allergenic: undeclared allergens or allergen cross-contact.

## Common pathogens (know source + control)
| Pathogen | Typical sources | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Salmonella | raw poultry, eggs, raw egg sauces/aioli, meat | cook to 75°C, avoid cross-contamination, pasteurised eggs in raw-egg products |
| Campylobacter | raw/undercooked poultry, unpasteurised milk | thorough cooking, separate boards |
| Listeria monocytogenes | soft cheeses, pâté, deli meats, smoked salmon, pre-cut salads, rockmelon | grows in the fridge; strict use-by, clean equipment; risk for pregnant women, elderly, immunocompromised |
| E. coli (STEC/EHEC) | undercooked mince, unpasteurised juice/milk, contaminated sprouts | cook mince to 75°C, wash produce |
| Staphylococcus aureus | humans (nose, skin, cuts) → handled foods; toxin not destroyed by reheating | hygiene, cover cuts, keep cold |
| Clostridium perfringens | large pots of stews, gravies, roasts cooled slowly | rapid cooling (2h/4h), small batches, reheat to 75°C |
| Bacillus cereus | cooked rice and pasta held warm; spores survive cooking | cool rice quickly, hold ≥60°C or ≤5°C |
| Clostridium botulinum | low-oxygen foods: garlic/herbs in oil, vacuum packs, poorly canned food | acidify/refrigerate infused oils, correct processes |
| Norovirus | sick food handlers, raw shellfish (oysters) | exclusion for 48 h after symptoms, hand washing |
| Hepatitis A | infected handlers, shellfish, frozen berries | hygiene, exclusion, reputable suppliers |
| Vibrio | raw seafood | keep cold, cook seafood |

## What bacteria need to grow (FATTOM)
Food (protein-rich, moist), Acidity (pH above ~4.6), Temperature (5–60°C), Time (doubling every 10–20 min), Oxygen, Moisture. Controls: temperature control, limit time, acidity, sugar, salt, drying.

## Personal hygiene requirements (Std 3.2.2 Division 4, cl 13–18)
- Wash hands: before starting work and handling food, after toilet, after handling raw meat/poultry/seafood, after touching hair/face/nose, smoking, eating, coughing, sneezing, using phone, handling waste or chemicals, cleaning, handling money, between raw and RTE food tasks, and before putting on gloves.
- Hand-wash basin: used only for hands, with warm running water, soap and single-use towels, easily accessible.
- Hand-washing steps: wet hands, apply soap, lather all surfaces (backs, between fingers, nails, wrists) for at least 20 seconds (guidance; the Code requires soap or similar, warm running water and a single-use towel — cl 15(4)), rinse, dry with paper towel, turn off tap with the towel.
- Clean uniform/apron; hair tied back and covered (hair net/hat); beard net if needed; short clean nails, no nail polish or false nails; minimal jewellery (plain band only usually); no strong perfume.
- Cover cuts and wounds with a dressing fully covered by a waterproof covering (cl 15(1)(d)) — a brightly coloured (blue) waterproof plaster, plus a glove.
- Don't eat, smoke, chew gum, spit or sneeze/cough over food or food-contact surfaces.
- Gloves: wash hands before putting on; change when torn, after 30–60 minutes of continuous use, between raw and RTE tasks, after touching face/rubbish; gloves are not a substitute for hand washing.
- Illness (cl 14): report to the supervisor a symptom (diarrhoea, vomiting, sore throat with fever, fever, jaundice), a foodborne disease or being a carrier, and do not handle food where it could be contaminated; also report a "condition" (infected skin lesion, discharge from ear, nose or eye) and tell the supervisor if you may have contaminated food. Health departments advise no food handling until 48 hours after symptoms stop.
- Report to supervisor: personal health issues, hygiene hazards (no soap, broken equipment), incidents of contamination, pest sightings.
- Sick procedure: phone (not text) the supervisor before the shift, say your symptoms, stay home; the supervisor records it and covers the shift. Come back only after 48 h with no symptoms (medical clearance if required, e.g. hepatitis A). "Feeling better" is not safe: you can still shed bacteria or viruses (e.g. norovirus) for days and pass them to food on your hands.

## More SITXFSA005/006 answers
- Unlabelled sanitiser bottle: don't use it; set it aside and tell the supervisor; dispose of it safely; refill a clean bottle from the original container and label it (product, dilution); SDS available. Why: unknown chemical or wrong strength → chemical contamination, poor sanitising, injury; WHS law requires hazardous chemical containers to be labelled.
- Business consequences of poor hygiene: customers sick (even deaths), fines and penalty notices, prosecution under the Food Act, improvement notices or closure orders, public listing (e.g. NSW Food Authority "Name and Shame"), legal claims, bad reviews and lost reputation, lost income and jobs.
- Thermometer ice-point check (FSANZ method). I fill a cup with crushed ice and a little cold water. The ice must not float. I put the probe in the centre, not touching the cup. After about 3 minutes it must read 0°C, within ±1°C (cl 22). I repeat up to three times, then adjust or replace it and record. How often: the Code sets no fixed time. I follow our food safety program. NSW Food Authority says at least every 6 months. Many kitchens check weekly or monthly. I also check after a drop, a knock or an odd reading. A wrong probe lets unsafe food pass. If it reads 6°C in ice slurry, it is 6°C too high. Hot food read as 60°C may be only 54°C. I stop using it, tag it, and recalibrate or replace it. I recheck the foods it measured, then record and report it.
- High-risk (vulnerable) customers: pregnant women, babies and young children, the elderly, people with weak immune systems (cancer treatment, HIV, transplants, diabetes), people who are ill or in hospital or aged care.
- Cool room order, top to bottom: cooked and ready-to-eat food, dairy and desserts; washed fruit and vegetables; raw seafood; raw whole meat cuts; raw mince and sausages; raw poultry at the bottom (needs the highest cooking temperature). All covered, labelled, on trays, off the floor.
- Single-use items (not reused — 3.2.2 cl 23): disposable gloves, cling film and foil, paper towels, piping bags, takeaway containers, cups, straws, skewers, disposable cutlery. Use once and bin; store covered. Reusing them is risky: they can't be cleaned and sanitised, can tear, and carry bacteria, allergens or chemicals to the next food.

## Food handling controls through the flow of food
1. Purchasing: approved/reputable suppliers only.
2. Receiving (cl 5): check temperature (PHF 5°C or below or 60°C or above; frozen food frozen), packaging intact, labels/use-by dates, signs of pests, product quality (smell, colour, texture), quantity matches the order; reject/record non-conforming goods; put away chilled and frozen goods first and quickly.
3. Storage (cl 6): cover, label (product, date prepared/received, use-by, initials), FIFO / FEFO rotation; raw food below cooked/RTE food in the fridge; store off the floor; separate chemicals from food; don't overload fridges; check and record fridge/freezer temperatures (at least daily, often twice).
4. Thawing: in fridge on a tray at the bottom; record; use within the safe time.
5. Preparation: minimise time in danger zone; prepare in small batches; separate raw and RTE preparation (colour-coded boards and knives, separate areas or times, clean and sanitise between tasks); wash fruit and vegetables.
6. Cooking (cl 7(1)(b)(ii)): to safe core temperatures (FSANZ guidance 75°C for poultry, mince, sausages, rolled and stuffed meats), measure with a clean sanitised probe in the thickest part.
7. Cooling (cl 7(3)): I split it into shallow trays or containers (about 5 cm deep), ice bath, stir, blast chiller; 60→21°C in 2 h then 21→5°C in 4 h. I probe the centre after 2 hours (21°C or below) and after 4 more hours (5°C or below). I record each time and temperature. Then I cover it, add a date label and put it in the fridge. If the food misses these times, it is unsafe and I throw it out. Reheating does not make it safe: toxins and many bacteria survive (Safe Food Australia).
8. Reheating to hold hot (cl 7(4)): I heat it fast to 60°C or above. FSANZ says within 2 hours. A 75°C core is recommended. I reheat once only, never in a bain-marie.
9. Holding/display (cl 8): hot ≥60°C, cold ≤5°C; sneeze guards; serving utensils for each dish; time-as-a-control (2h/4h rule) if out of temperature control.
10. Serving/transport (cl 10): hold correct temperatures, insulated containers, covered, clean vehicles.
11. Disposal (cl 11): discard food that is contaminated, out of date, recalled, temperature-abused or returned from a customer's plate. Never re-serve food returned from a table (except unopened packaged items).

## Anaphylaxis
- Anaphylaxis = severe, life-threatening allergic reaction (swelling of throat/tongue, difficulty breathing, wheeze, collapse). Response: call 000, give adrenaline autoinjector (EpiPen) if available, lay person flat (or sit if breathing is difficult), do not let them stand or walk.

## Food Standards Code obligations most often asked
- Std 3.2.2: skills and knowledge for food handlers (cl 3); notify the local council before operating (cl 4); receive, store, process, display, package, transport, dispose and recall food safely (cl 5–12); health and hygiene (cl 13–18); cleanliness, cleaning and sanitising, maintenance (cl 19–21); thermometer accurate to ±1°C (cl 22); single-use items not reused (cl 23); animals and pests excluded (cl 24).
- Std 3.2.2A Food Safety Management Tools (from 8 Dec 2023), for caterers, food service and retailers handling unpackaged PHF that is ready to eat:
  - Category one (cl 6: caterer or food service that makes unpackaged PHF into ready-to-eat PHF — restaurants, cafes, caterers): all three tools (cl 8) — food handler training (cl 10), a food safety supervisor (cl 11) and substantiation records (cl 12).
  - Category two (cl 7: sells unpackaged ready-to-eat PHF it did not make, e.g. delis, service stations): FSS + food handler training (cl 9).
  - Food handler training (cl 10): before the task, a food safety training course or skills and knowledge matching it (safe handling, contamination, cleaning and sanitising, personal hygiene).
  - FSS (cl 2, 11): certificate from an RTO or recognised organisation issued within the last 5 years, authority to direct staff, appointed before handling starts and reasonably available.
  - Substantiation (cl 12): records of receipt, storage, display and transport temperatures, cooking, minimising time, cooling, reheating, and cleaning and sanitising; kept 3 months (cl 12(2)) unless compliance is shown another way (cl 12(3)).
- Std 3.2.3: premises and equipment designed to be easily cleaned, adequate water supply, hand-wash facilities, ventilation, lighting, pest proofing, toilets, storage, waste and sewage disposal.

## HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) — 7 principles
1. Conduct a hazard analysis.
2. Identify critical control points (CCPs) — steps where control is essential (e.g. cooking, cooling, cold storage).
3. Set critical limits (e.g. 75°C core; ≤5°C).
4. Establish monitoring procedures (probe and record).
5. Establish corrective actions (e.g. continue cooking, discard, adjust fridge).
6. Establish verification procedures (audits, calibration, review of records).
7. Establish record keeping and documentation.
Typical records: goods received log, fridge/freezer temperature log, cooking/cooling/reheating logs, cleaning schedule sign-off, pest control reports, thermometer calibration log, staff training records, supplier list, recall procedure.

## Food recall
Follow the recall plan and FSANZ notices: isolate affected stock, label "Do not use – recall", return or dispose as directed, record.
